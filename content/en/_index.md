### 1. [Transplanted Immune Cells Donate Mitochondria to Neighbors, Improving Energy Metabolism](https://lifespan.io/transplanted-immune-cells-donate-mitochondria-to-neighbors/)
**Transplanted immune cells donate mitochondria to neighbors**—mouse studies show that after bone marrow transplant, healthy immune cells not only replace diseased cells but also transfer mitochondria to surrounding neurons and cardiomyocytes. Recipients' mitochondrial respiration partially recovered, motor coordination improved, and cardiac pumping function increased ~50%. Recovery was incomplete, though: recipient cell performance remained between diseased and healthy levels. This is a mouse model of Friedreich's ataxia, a rare disease; human application requires further validation.

**Source type**: Peer-reviewed paper (Nature Communications) / Evidence stage: Animal study / Credibility: High

### 2. [Mitochondrial Health May Strongly Influence Learning Ability in the Elderly](https://lifespan.io/mitochondrial-performance-may-strongly-influence-learning/)
**Mitochondrial performance may strongly influence learning**—researchers trained mice on a rule then suddenly changed it. Aged mice's learning flexibility correlated directly with presynaptic mitochondrial function, not with conventional aging markers. After 20 weeks of mitochondrial antioxidant MitoQ, older mice adapted to new rules significantly faster. This suggests mitochondrial reactive oxygen species may impair neuronal structural plasticity. The study has not yet identified specific intervention targets; clinical translation needs further work.

**Source type**: Science news / Evidence stage: Animal study / Credibility: Medium

### 3. [Faster Biological Aging May Explain Rising Cancer Incidence Under Age 55](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260913082200.htm)
**Accelerated biological aging and early-onset cancer**—researchers found that recent generations are biologically older at the same chronological age. Accelerated biological age correlated with higher early-onset cancer risk, especially lung, gastrointestinal, uterine, and colorectal cancers. The study didn't specify sample size, biological age measurement method, or causality. This is an observational association; no intervention target has been established yet.

**Source type**: Science news / Evidence stage: Epidemiological observation / Credibility: Medium
