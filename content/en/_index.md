### 1. Gut Bacteria's Odd-Chain Fatty Acids Can Suppress Atherosclerosis in Mice

**Bacteroides uniformis** and its secreted **C15:0** (pentadecanoic acid, an odd-chain saturated fatty acid) are the stars of a mouse study published in *Nature*. According to a [Lifespan.io report](https://lifespan.io/gut-bacteria-molecule-slows-atherosclerosis-in-mice/), this gut bacterium and its metabolite can inhibit liver HMG-CoA reductase and upregulate LDL receptors, thereby lowering blood LDL cholesterol and shrinking aortic plaque area by roughly 50%. Atorvastatin served as the positive control, with comparable or slightly superior results. The study also detected significantly low C15:0 levels in dyslipidemia patients and re-analyzed existing microbiome sequencing datasets. **All current results come from Apoe gene-knockout mouse models** 🧭; the experiments compared endpoint differences between treatment and vehicle control groups rather than baselines, so they can only demonstrate slowing — not reversing — of plaque; whether C15:0 as a commercially available supplement outperforms statins currently has zero human data to back it up.

### 2. Protective Brain Cells Gone Rogue May Drive Cognitive Decline

**Protective brain cells** — previously thought to be entirely beneficial — may actually drive cognitive decline when they go off the rails 🧠. According to a [Science Daily report](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261015073222.htm), scientists discovered that these cells, when functioning abnormally, can produce excess myelin that disrupts nerve fiber conduction, potentially linked to declines in memory and thinking. This research direction is directly relevant to the pathological mechanisms behind aging-related cognitive decline and dementia. **Currently only abstract-level info is available** ⚠️; the study subjects, sample sizes, experimental types, and specific values all await full-text verification, and you can't infer intervention effects or clinical significance from the existing abstract alone.
