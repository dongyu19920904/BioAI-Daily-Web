### 1. Brain Serotonin Signals Increase with Age, Actively Suppressing Physical Activity in Aging Mice

According to [a report from Lifespan.io](https://lifespan.io/serotonin-signals-suppress-physical-activity-in-aging-mice/), a study from the University of Aberdeen published in *Metabolism* found that serotonin-producing neurons in the brainstems of aging mice fire more frequently. Serotonin is a neurotransmitter that affects mood and movement, and the increased signaling suppresses dopamine release in another brain region — dopamine being tied to motivation and physical activity — causing mice to voluntarily move less. When researchers used a virus to selectively destroy those serotonin neurons, mice aged 14–20 months showed increased activity, reduced body fat, and stronger forelimb grip strength over eight weeks, with no significant change in food intake. This suggests that age-related activity decline may be partly a brain "putting on the brakes" rather than muscle weakness. The results are mouse-only for now, and directly destroying neurons isn't applicable to humans, so future research will need to find gentler ways to intervene. 🧠

### 2. Cambrian Bio's AMPK Activator Shows Metabolic Activity Signals in 23 Participants

According to [a report from Lifespan.io](https://lifespan.io/james-peyer-on-turning-aging-biology-into-medicine/), Cambrian Bio's candidate drug ATX-304 was tested in an early exploratory four-month human trial with 23 participants. At roughly half the dose that produced weight-loss effects in animals, the trial observed an approximately 8% increase in resting metabolic rate, a doubling of adiponectin (a metabolically active signaling protein secreted by fat tissue), and statistically significant reductions in triglycerides, liver fat, and visceral fat. AMPK is a key cellular switch for sensing energy status — activating it simultaneously promotes glucose uptake and mitochondrial energy production, theoretically mimicking the effects of exercise. The trial was small and exploratory, long-term safety data don't yet exist, and the drug is now entering Phase 2 clinical trials. 💊

### 3. D&D Pharmatech's Liver Drug Resolves MASH in 48-Week Biopsy Data

According to [a report from Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/dd-pharmatechs-liver-drug-resolves-mash-in-62-5-of-patients/), D&D Pharmatech has released 48-week liver biopsy data for its GLP-1-class drug zabopegdutide, showing MASH resolution in 62.5% of participating patients. MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis) is a chronic liver disease that can progress to cirrhosis. Liver biopsy is the current gold standard for assessing fatty liver drug efficacy, requiring tissue samples confirmed under a microscope. The company says it's in licensing talks with major pharma firms. The source material doesn't provide full trial size or control group design details, so specific numbers should be verified against the original report. 🔬

### 4. The Senescence Field Is Heading to Stanford to Work Out a Unified Measurement Standard

According to [a report from Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/senescence-field-heads-to-stanford-to-settle-what-counts-as-proof/), the Senescent Cell Biomarker Consortium (SBC) will hold its inaugural summit at Stanford University on December 7th, bringing together academia, pharmaceutical companies, and regulators to discuss standardization. Senescent cells are cells that stop dividing but remain in the body, continuously releasing inflammatory signals, and are thought to be linked to multiple age-related diseases. They can be detected fairly reliably in mice, but there's no consensus yet on how to reliably identify and measure them in human patients. Without shared measurement standards, you simply can't compare the efficacy of drugs in the same class side-by-side — and that's the core bottleneck blocking the whole field from advancing clinically. 🏛️

### 5. Annovis's Alzheimer's Drug Wins Patent Protection Through 2044

According to [a report from Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/annovis-wins-patent-on-alzheimers-drug-through-2044/), Annovis Bio has received a U.S. patent protecting a novel crystalline form of its candidate drug buntanetap through 2044. Different crystal forms are treated as distinct products under patent law even when the active ingredient is the same, so this patent covers the specific version being used in the company's ongoing pivotal Phase 3 trial. Topline data from that trial are expected soon. The patent has nothing to do with efficacy and doesn't mean the drug has been approved for patients. 📋

### 6. VectorY Raises $83.5M to Push Its ALS Antibody Into Larger Trials

According to [a report from Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/vectory-raises-83-5m-to-push-its-als-antibody/), Amsterdam and Boston biotech VectorY Therapeutics announced on November 11th the close of an $83.5 million (approximately €74.5 million) funding round to expand clinical trials for its ALS antibody drug. The funding announcement didn't disclose the current trial phase or enrollment numbers, and efficacy data are pending. 💉

Epigenetic clocks estimate biological age (the degree of cellular aging, which can differ from chronological age) by detecting DNA methylation patterns. ## **❓ Related Questions**

### Do the human trial results for the AMPK activator prove it's effective against aging?

Not yet. The 23-person, four-month exploratory trial mentioned in [the Lifespan.io report](https://lifespan.io/james-peyer-on-turning-aging-biology-into-medicine/) measured metabolic markers — resting metabolic rate, fat metabolism proteins, liver fat, etc. Those are early signals of safety and biological activity, not outcome measures for aging or healthspan. The trial wasn't designed to demonstrate lifespan extension. ATX-304 is currently entering Phase 2 clinical trials with obesity as the primary indication. The exercise endurance doubling and lifespan extension seen in animal studies haven't been replicated in humans yet. 🔎
