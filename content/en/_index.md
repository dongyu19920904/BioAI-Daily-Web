## **🔥 Today's Top 2 Highlights**

### 1. A Blood Test May Warn of Alzheimer's Risk Up to a Decade Before Symptoms Show

A large-scale international study, as covered in a [ScienceDaily media report](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261029072142.htm), found that a simple blood test can flag high-risk individuals years before cognitive decline sets in — those at highest risk had roughly a 38% chance of developing cognitive impairment within five years, and about 78% within ten, suggesting the test could open a window for early intervention. 🧪 Since this is a media summary, key details like the specific biomarkers tested and sample size aren't mentioned — the original paper needs to be tracked down for confirmation.

[![Source image: A simple blood test could reveal Alzheimer's risk 10 years before symptoms](https://www.sciencedaily.com/images/1920/alzheimers-disease-blood-test-tube.webp)](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261029072142.htm)

### 2. Aging Reshapes the Distribution and Stability of Memory Engram Cells

A paper published in [*Nature Communications*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-79049-9) found that aging changes "memory engram cells" — the neurons in the brain that store specific memories — in terms of their spatial distribution, stability, and gene expression profiles. 🧠 This suggests the physical foundation of memory storage itself may shift with age, not just the recall process. The paper is peer-reviewed, but only the title is available right now, so the experimental subjects, sample size, and quantitative findings all need a closer look at the full text.
