### 1. [Intermittent Fasting Shows Promise in a Huntington's Trial](https://lifespan.io/intermittent-fasting-shows-promise-in-a-huntingtons-trial/)

Huntington's disease was previously considered untreatable. Oregon Health & Science University's trial had 20 early-stage patients eat only within a 6-8 hour window daily for 12 weeks. Results: disease composite score improved by 0.50 points (typically declines 1 point per year), plasma neurofilament light chain (a neural damage marker) dropped an average of 12.6%, and mitochondrial respiration plus ATP production in peripheral blood mononuclear cells rose significantly. The study also found patients maintained body weight, dispelling concerns that fasting might worsen weight loss. However, this is a small trial without a control group (20 people), and participants simultaneously received nutritional guidance and exercise advice—improvements could stem from multiple factors.

**Source Type: Peer-reviewed paper (Nature Metabolism) / Early clinical trial (20 participants, no control) / Credibility: Medium**

### 2. [MIT Researchers Develop Noninvasive Way to Spot Aging Zombie Cells](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260921/MIT-researchers-develop-noninvasive-way-to-spot-aging-zombie-cells.aspx)

Senescent cells no longer divide but refuse to die; when accumulated, they trigger cancer, tissue degeneration, and inflammation. MIT researchers developed a noninvasive technique to locate these cells in the body. Previously, detection required biopsy or invasive methods; the new technique opens a window for monitoring senescent cell burden and evaluating clearance therapy effectiveness. However, the report doesn't disclose the technical principle, validation stage, or clinical feasibility—currently, we can only confirm this is a research-stage tool.

**Source Type: Media report / Research tool development (specific stage unknown) / Credibility: Medium**

### 3. [Precure Selects Helix as Multiomics Lab and AI Tools Provider](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/omics/precure-selects-helix-as-multiomics-lab-and-ai-tools-provider/)

Mayo Clinic launched the Precure initiative last week, aiming to generate molecular data from approximately 1 million biological samples and integrate genomic insights into routine clinical practice. Helix will provide clinical-grade whole genome sequencing (WGS), research-grade proteomics sequencing, and AI tools, using Ultima Genomics' UG200 sequencing platform. This continues Mayo's Tapestry study (already enrolling 100,000 people), but the complete chain from sample collection to clinical application still requires years of validation. Helix joins as a minority stakeholder; Mayo's financial returns will be reinvested in research and education.

**Source Type: Official announcement / Infrastructure build (data generation phase) / Credibility: High**

### 4. [Andelyn Curator Biomanufacturing Platform Supports Fayuvi Gene Therapy Approval](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/omics/andelyn-curator-biomanufacturing-platform-supports-fayuvi-gene-therapy-approval/)

FDA approved Ultragenyx's Fayuvi gene therapy last week for treating a rare fatal disease—Sanfilippo syndrome type A (MPS IIIA). This is the first AAV gene therapy manufactured using Andelyn's AAV Curator platform process to receive approval. MPS IIIA affects approximately 3,000-5,000 patients globally with a median lifespan of 15 years; no approved therapy existed previously. Andelyn's Columbus, Ohio facility has entered commercial production, with Ultragenyx's Bedford, Massachusetts facility producing simultaneously. This marks further maturation of manufacturing capacity and regulatory pathways for rare disease gene therapies.

**Source Type: Official announcement / FDA-approved product (commercialized) / Credibility: High**

### 5. [High Adolescent BMI Linked to Faster Biological Aging in Adulthood](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260921/High-adolescent-BMI-linked-to-faster-biological-aging-in-adulthood.aspx)

University of Jyväskylä in Finland research found that people with higher BMI during adolescence may biologically age faster than their chronological age in adulthood. The study, published in the International Journal of Obesity, tracked the relationship between adolescent BMI and adult epigenetic clocks (molecular markers measuring biological aging). This suggests adolescent weight management may impact long-term healthspan, but the study doesn't specify sample size, tracking duration, causal mechanisms, or whether confounding factors like diet and exercise were excluded.

**Source Type: Peer-reviewed paper / Observational study / Credibility: Medium**

### 6. [Survey: Older Adults Show Strong Interest in Dementia Vaccine](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-09-survey-older-adults-strong-dementia.html)

A new survey from USC's Schaeffer Center for Health Policy & Economics shows the vast majority of older adults express interest in receiving a vaccine that could significantly reduce dementia risk. This reflects public demand for preventive interventions, but no approved dementia vaccine currently exists. The survey doesn't reveal respondent numbers, willingness to pay, or acceptance of side effects. From intention to actual vaccination, multiple barriers remain: vaccine development, clinical trials, pricing, and insurance coverage.

**Source Type: Survey report / Public opinion survey / Credibility: Medium**
