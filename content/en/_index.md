## **🔥 Today's Top 8 Highlights**

### 1. One Shot, Two Years of Cholesterol Control — Non-Human Primate Data Is In

Scribe Therapeutics dropped new data at the American Heart Association (AHA) annual meeting: their experimental gene-editing therapy STX-1150, in a single injection, kept LDL cholesterol (the "bad" kind) at reduced levels for at least two years in non-human primates. Per [Longevity.Technology's coverage](https://longevity.technology/news/scribes-one-dose-approach-keeps-cholesterol-down-for-years/), the company also unveiled early data on a second candidate, STX-1200, at the same session. All results are from animal studies — no human clinical trials yet. 🧬

### 2. Elamipretide Slows Ovarian Aging in Mice — But Don't Get Too Excited Yet

Published in peer-reviewed journal *Aging Cell*, [this study](https://lifespan.io/the-short-peptide-elamipretide-delays-ovarian-aging-in-mice/) found that seven consecutive days of injecting short peptide Elamipretide into aging mice improved egg cell (oocyte) maturation quality, boosted fertilization rates, and increased litter sizes within one month post-treatment. The researchers also spotted similar improvement signals in cultured oocytes from women over 35 — but that's in vitro, not a clinical trial. There's still a big gap between lab dishes and actual human fertility outcomes. 🔬

![Elamipretide effects](https://lifespan.io/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Elamipretide-effects.png)

### 3. Yogurt and Cheese Probably Won't Save Your Aging Muscles After 50

A cross-sectional analysis of 6,790 Irish adults aged 50+ found that — after controlling for lifestyle and socioeconomic factors — eating fermented dairy (yogurt and cheese) showed no consistent link to grip strength, physical performance, or frailty levels. Per [News-Medical's report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261111/Yogurt-and-cheese-are-rich-in-protein-but-do-they-help-aging-muscles.aspx), one analysis even found a slight association between higher dairy intake and worse grip strength odds, though other analyses couldn't replicate that finding. Classic caveat: cross-sectional studies can only show correlation at a single point in time, not causation. The researchers are calling for longitudinal follow-up. 🥛

### 4. Turning Regular Skin Into Tough Palm Tissue — Phase 1 Trial Fully Enrolled

Johns Hopkins Medicine researchers, in an NIH-funded Phase 1 trial, have partially converted ordinary skin cells into "palmar skin" — the kind of durable, friction-resistant tissue found on palms and the soles of feet. Per [News-Medical's coverage](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261111/Experimental-cell-therapy-helps-ordinary-skin-mimic-tough-palm-tissue.aspx), Phase 1 is primarily a safety evaluation, so no efficacy data has been released yet. Specific enrollment numbers and completion details are pending verification from the original source. 🩺

### 5. A Rare Gene Mutation Might Protect Against Parkinson's and Lewy Body Dementia

Oregon Health & Science University researchers identified a rare genetic mutation that may offer protection against both Parkinson's disease and Lewy body dementia — a type of dementia characterized by abnormal protein deposits in the brain. According to [News-Medical's report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261111/Rare-genetic-mutation-may-protect-against-Parkinsons-and-Lewy-body-dementia.aspx), the current source is abstract-level, so the specific study design, sample size, and mutation locus all need verification from the full paper. This does not mean the mutation can be artificially replicated or used therapeutically — at least not yet. 🧠

### 6. Gut Microbial Metabolites May Reshape Fat Tissue Through Wnt Signaling

[This study reported by News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261111/Gut-microbial-metabolites-may-reshape-fat-tissue-through-Wnt-pathways.aspx) looks at how metabolites produced by gut bacteria influence adipose tissue — specifically through the Wnt signaling pathway, a mechanism that controls cell proliferation and differentiation. The abstract suggests that when fat tissue can't generate new fat cells fast enough, excess energy piles up in aging adipocytes, potentially triggering inflammation and insulin resistance. Source is abstract-level; study subjects, sample size, and specific results need verification from the full paper. 🦠

## **⌘ Top Open Source Projects**

### qlu-lab/SARDINE: A Statistical Framework for Studying Aging Phenotype Associations Using Epigenetic Clocks

[SARDINE](https://github.com/qlu-lab/SARDINE) is an R framework for estimating associations between "epigenetic clocks" and various aging phenotypes using summary statistics from epigenome-wide association studies (EWAS). Epigenetic clocks are tools that estimate biological age via DNA methylation patterns. SARDINE's core value is that it works with publicly available summary statistics — no individual-level data needed, making it accessible to researchers without large raw cohorts. Last active in early November 2026, with 1 star. Check the repo's docs and examples before diving in.

### anbai106/SleepChart: Modeling Scripts for Sleep Duration vs. 23 Biological Age Clocks

[SleepChart](https://github.com/anbai106/SleepChart) provides R scripts for analyzing the relationship between sleep duration and 23 biological age clocks, using Generalized Additive Models (GAM — a statistical method that captures non-linear relationships). It's built for data analysts working at the intersection of sleep and aging research. The repo includes the original analysis scripts, has 3 stars, and was last active in late October 2026. Check the repo page for license info before using.

## **❓ Related Questions**

### What Are Elamipretide's Human Trial Results — Can It Be Used Now?

Not for clinical use yet. The human component in [this *Aging Cell* study](https://lifespan.io/the-short-peptide-elamipretide-delays-ovarian-aging-in-mice/) was limited to in vitro cultured oocytes — not a human clinical trial. The in-vivo mouse results also can't be directly extrapolated to humans. Elamipretide has been studied in humans for heart failure before, but there's no registered clinical trial data available for the ovarian aging indication. 🔬
