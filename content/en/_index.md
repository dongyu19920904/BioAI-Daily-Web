### 1. Shift Bioscience publishes Nature Biotechnology paper, delivering new scoring framework for AI "virtual cells"

[Longevity.Technology reports](https://longevity.technology/news/shift-fixes-how-ai-virtual-cells-get-graded/) that **Shift Bioscience**, the Cambridge-based company, announced on October 5 the publication of a paper in *Nature Biotechnology* on a calibration framework for genetic perturbation models. The paper points out that poor test scores for virtual-cell models may partly stem from bias in the evaluation metrics themselves, not model inadequacy. After correcting the scoring system, some models displayed biologically meaningful predictive capability. Shift stated it will apply the framework to aging target screening, prioritizing a combined in-vitro and computational screen starting with **liver fibrosis**. Current results are a methodological improvement; no new targets or human data exist yet. A better scoring ruler does not equal drug efficacy.

### 2. KAUST and Oxford jointly develop Parkinson's blood sensor, 90.9% accuracy in small sample

[Longevity.Technology reports](https://longevity.technology/news/blood-sensor-hunts-parkinsons-signals-in-40-minutes/) that in a study published in *Science Advances*, teams from **King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST)** and the University of Oxford used an organic electrochemical transistor (OECT) sensor to read three forms of **alpha-synuclein** from blood simultaneously in approximately 40 minutes. In blind evaluation of the Oxford Discovery cohort comprising **59 participants** (Parkinson's patients, healthy controls, and isolated REM sleep behavior disorder patients), the platform achieved 90.9% accuracy. Researchers explicitly noted this is not yet a standalone clinical blood test; it is a retrospective, small-sample pilot study. False-positive/false-negative analysis was not disclosed; larger-scale prospective multicenter validation is needed.

### 3. Insilico Medicine nominates AI-designed oral VAV1 degrader as preclinical candidate

[Longevity.Technology reports](https://longevity.technology/news/insilicos-ai-built-pill-cuts-colitis-scores-by-60-in-animal-tests/) that **Insilico Medicine** announced the nomination of **ISM9546** as a preclinical candidate—an oral VAV1 molecular glue degrader designed with the Chemistry42 platform, targeting autoimmune diseases. VAV1 has long been considered "undruggable" due to the absence of a clear drug-binding pocket. In an animal acute colitis model, histopathological scores improved by approximately **60%**, with reductions in inflammatory cell infiltration and intestinal damage; 14-day toxicology studies in rodents and nonhuman primates showed no significant safety signals. All current data come from animal experiments; human data and clinical entry timeline have not been disclosed.

### 4. Low-fat vegan diet 16-week clinical trial: Type 2 diabetes patients average 33% reduction in liver fat

[Lifespan.io reports](https://lifespan.io/low-fat-vegan-diet-shows-benefits-in-type-2-diabetes/) that a single-arm clinical trial published in *BMJ Open Diabetes Research & Care* enrolled **24 completers** (Type 2 diabetes patients, mean age 59) for a 16-week ad-libitum low-fat vegan diet. **HbA1c dropped from 7.6% to 6.6%**, liver fat decreased by approximately 33%, visceral fat volume fell by about 290 cm³, and skin advanced glycation end-product (AGE) score declined from 3.1 to 2.7. Some participants reduced or stopped metformin. This is the first plant-based dietary intervention study to simultaneously measure liver fat, muscle fat, visceral fat, and skin AGE. It lacks a control group, has a small sample size, and participants naturally reduced calorie intake by 346 kcal/day and lost 6 kg body weight, so results cannot rule out the confounding contribution of an energy deficit.

### 5. Roche licenses Rona Therapeutics' ALK7 RNAi therapy

[Longevity.Technology reports](https://longevity.technology/news/roche-licenses-ronas-rnai-drug-that-targets-fat-cells/) that **Rona Therapeutics** announced on October 13 an exclusive global license to Roche for an RNAi candidate therapy targeting **ALK7** (encoded by the ACVR1C gene), for obesity and metabolic diseases. Potential milestone payments are capped at **$100 million** upfront and near-term plus **$1.06 billion** downstream. ALK7 is predominantly expressed in adipose tissue, and human genetics research has linked lower ALK7 activity to healthier fat distribution. The announcement provides no human data; milestone totals are paid only if the drug advances through each stage, not representing actual revenue.

### 6. Italy's San Raffaele Hospital launches early lab study on encapsulated pancreatic β cells

[Longevity.Technology reports](https://longevity.technology/news/italian-lab-to-test-cell-capsules-for-diabetes/) that **Avaí Bio** and **Austrianova** announced on October 7 the first joint steering committee meeting with **Ospedale San Raffaele (OSR)** to initiate a preliminary study protocol for **encapsulated β-cell replacement therapy** for insulin-dependent diabetes. OSR provides a stem-cell-derived β-cell differentiation platform; Austrianova provides Cell-in-a-Box® encapsulation technology. The research's primary goal is to confirm whether encapsulated cells can maintain viability and function; no patients are involved yet. OSR's Professor Lorenzo Piemonti explicitly stated the work is at an extremely early stage; there are no in-vitro or animal data available for citation.
