## **🧬 Today's Top 2 Highlights**

### 1. Gut Bacteria-Produced Odd-Chain Fatty Acids Can Suppress Atherosclerosis in Mice

**Bacteroides uniformis** — a gut bacterium — and the **C15:0** (pentadecanoic acid, an odd-chain saturated fatty acid) it pumps out can hit the brakes on liver HMG-CoA reductase and ramp up LDL receptors, slashing blood LDL cholesterol and shrinking aortic plaque area by about 50%. That's the headline from a mouse study published in *Nature*, per [a Lifespan.io report](https://lifespan.io/gut-bacteria-molecule-slows-atherosclerosis-in-mice/). Atorvastatin was used as a positive control and performed comparably or slightly better. The researchers also found that C15:0 levels were notably low in people with dyslipidemia, and they reanalyzed existing microbiome sequencing datasets. **Every bit of these results comes from Apoe knockout mouse models** — the experiments compared treatment groups against vehicle controls at endpoint, not against baseline, so they show slowed plaque growth, not reversal. And as for whether C15:0 supplements beat statins? No human data to back that claim yet.

### 2. Protective Brain Cell Dysfunction May Drive Cognitive Decline

A type of brain cell previously thought to be entirely beneficial can, when it goes haywire, crank out abnormal amounts of myelin that mess with nerve fiber conduction — potentially linked to declines in memory and thinking ability. That's what scientists discovered, according to [a Science Daily report](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261015073222.htm). This line of research ties directly into the pathological mechanisms behind aging-related cognitive decline and dementia. **Right now we're working with abstract-level info only** — the study subjects, sample sizes, experiment types, and specific values all need to be verified against the full paper, and you can't draw conclusions about intervention effects or clinical significance from the abstract alone.
