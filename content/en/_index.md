### 1. Shift Bioscience publishes Nature Biotechnology paper offering new scoring framework for AI "virtual cells"

**Shift Bioscience**, a Cambridge-based company, announced on October 5 the publication of a paper in *Nature Biotechnology* on a calibration framework for genetic perturbation models, [according to Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/shift-fixes-how-ai-virtual-cells-get-graded/). The paper argues that poor performance scores of prior virtual-cell models may stem partly from bias in the evaluation metrics themselves, not just model shortcomings. After correcting the scoring system, some models demonstrated biologically meaningful predictive capability. Shift says it will apply the framework to aging-target screening, starting with **liver fibrosis** through combined in-vitro and computational screening. Current results are methodological improvements with no new targets or human data; a better grading ruler doesn't guarantee drug efficacy.

### 2. KAUST and Oxford jointly develop Parkinson's blood sensor with 90.9% accuracy in small cohort

**KAUST** (King Abdullah University of Science and Technology) and the University of Oxford used an organic electrochemical transistor (OECT) sensor to simultaneously read three forms of **alpha-synuclein** from blood in about 40 minutes, in research published in *Science Advances* [reported by Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/blood-sensor-hunts-parkinsons-signals-in-40-minutes/). In a blinded evaluation of the Oxford Discovery cohort—**59 participants** (Parkinson's patients, healthy controls, and individuals with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder)—the platform achieved 90.9% accuracy. Researchers explicitly noted this is not yet an independent clinical blood test; it's a retrospective, small-cohort pilot study with no false-positive/false-negative breakdown disclosed and requiring larger prospective multicenter validation.

### 3. Insilico Medicine nominates AI-designed oral VAV1 degrader as preclinical candidate

**Insilico Medicine** announced the nomination of **ISM9546**, an oral VAV1 molecular glue degrader designed with its Chemistry42 platform, as a preclinical candidate targeting autoimmune diseases, [according to Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/insilicos-ai-built-pill-cuts-colitis-scores-by-60-in-animal-tests/). VAV1 has long been considered "undruggable" due to the lack of obvious binding pockets. In animal acute colitis models, histopathological scores improved by approximately **60%**, with reduced inflammatory cell infiltration and intestinal damage; 14-day toxicology studies in rodents and non-human primates showed no apparent safety signals. All current data come from animal experiments; human data and clinical entry timelines have not been disclosed.

### 4. Low-fat vegan diet in 16-week clinical trial: type 2 diabetes patients show average 33% reduction in liver fat

A single-arm clinical trial published in *BMJ Open Diabetes Research & Care* enrolled **24 completers** (type 2 diabetes patients, mean age 59) on a 16-week ad-libitum low-fat vegan diet, [according to Lifespan.io](https://lifespan.io/low-fat-vegan-diet-shows-benefits-in-type-2-diabetes/). **HbA1c fell from 7.6% to 6.6%**, liver fat decreased by about 33%, visceral fat volume dropped by roughly 290 cm³, and skin advanced glycation end-product (AGE) scores fell from 3.1 to 2.7. Some participants reduced or stopped metformin. This is the first plant-based dietary intervention study to simultaneously measure liver fat, muscle fat, visceral fat, and skin AGE. With no control group and a small sample, participants naturally reduced calorie intake by 346 kcal/day and lost 6 kg, so results cannot exclude the confounding contribution of an energy deficit.

### 5. Roche licenses Rona Therapeutics' ALK7 RNAi therapy

**Rona Therapeutics** announced on October 13 that it granted Roche exclusive worldwide rights to an RNAi candidate targeting **ALK7** (encoded by the ACVR1C gene) for obesity and metabolic diseases, [according to Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/roche-licenses-ronas-rnai-drug-that-targets-fat-cells/). Potential milestone payments cap at **$100 million** upfront/near-term plus **$1.06 billion** downstream. ALK7 is predominantly expressed in adipose tissue, and human genetics studies associate lower ALK7 activity with healthier fat distribution. The announcement contains no human data; milestone totals are paid only if the drug progresses through each phase and do not represent actual revenue.

### 6. Italy's San Raffaele hospital launches early lab study on encapsulated pancreatic β-cells

**Avaí Bio** and **Austrianova** announced on October 7 their first joint steering-committee meeting with Ospedale San Raffaele (OSR) to initiate a preliminary research protocol for **encapsulated β-cell replacement therapy** in insulin-dependent diabetes, [according to Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/italian-lab-to-test-cell-capsules-for-diabetes/). OSR provides a stem-cell-derived β-cell differentiation platform; Austrianova supplies Cell-in-a-Box® encapsulation technology. The study's primary goal is to confirm whether encapsulated cells can maintain viability and function; no patients are involved yet. OSR's Professor Lorenzo Piemonti explicitly stated this is very early-stage work; no in-vitro or animal data are available for citation.
