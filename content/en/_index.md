## **🔥 Top 10 Headlines**

### [Deep Learning Sepsis Prediction Models Collapse on Multi-Center ICU Data](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02364-4)
Sepsis prediction AI in ICUs achieved 90%+ accuracy at training hospitals, then tanked to 60% at new ones. A research team tested across 6 medical centers with 35 matched samples and discovered these models are heavily dependent on specific hospital data distributions—move to a new environment and they completely fail. This exposes a brutal truth: AI that scores high in the lab becomes a "paper tiger" in real clinical settings. Medical AI's generalization ability is far more fragile than we imagined.

### [ChatGPT Wants to Be a Therapist? Study Finds It Commits 15 Types of Ethical Errors](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/03/260302030642.htm)
Millions are turning to ChatGPT for mental health advice, but Brown University's new research throws cold water on that idea. Researchers had ChatGPT simulate a psychotherapist and found it misses crisis situations, reinforces harmful beliefs, displays bias, and delivers "false empathy"—seeming to care while actually not understanding your situation at all. Worse, even with explicit instructions to follow professional standards, these problems persist. AI can be a tool, but don't expect it to replace a real therapist.

### [AI "Super Hearing" in Emergency Calls: Real-Time Cardiac Arrest Detection](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02498-5)
In emergency calls where every second counts, deep language models are becoming dispatchers' "second set of ears." This system analyzes call content in real time, identifying cardiac arrest signs while dispatchers are still taking the initial report—not waiting for the call to end, but judging as it happens. This "listen-and-diagnose" capability could rewrite the rules of pre-hospital emergency response.

### [Self-Supervised Learning Makes Arrhythmia Detection More Reliable](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-41549-5)
The biggest pain point in cardiac AI? Expensive data annotation and poor model generalization. This research combines self-supervised learning with 1D Vision Transformers, letting models "teach themselves" from massive unlabeled ECG data before fine-tuning with limited labeled samples. The result: stable accuracy across different hospitals and equipment. This "low-annotation, strong-generalization" approach could be the key to getting medical AI into clinical practice.

### [AI Drug Design: Sorafenib Derivatives Target VEGFR2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-41232-9)
Sorafenib is a classic anti-angiogenesis drug, but it has major side effects and rapid resistance. A research team used AI molecular fragment optimization to design new sorafenib derivatives specifically targeting VEGFR2. Computer simulations show these new molecules have stronger binding affinity and better pharmacokinetics. While still in silico, this "AI-repurposing old drugs" approach could accelerate new drug development.

### [Generative Models Help You "Translate" Genes Across Species](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-69966-0)
Want to express human genes in mice? Direct copy-paste won't work—different species have vastly different gene expression mechanisms. This research uses generative models combined with orthologous gene information to automatically "translate" gene sequences so they work properly in target species. This is huge for synthetic biology and cross-species research—soon you might not need manual codon optimization for cross-species experiments.

### [Multimodal AI Predicts Cardiopulmonary Exercise Test Performance](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02493-w)
Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) is the gold standard for assessing heart-lung function, but it's exhausting and time-consuming. This multimodal multi-instance learning model integrates patient clinical data, imaging, and lab results to predict CPET outcomes in advance. For patients who can't complete full testing (elderly, critically ill), this AI provides an alternative assessment. Saves time and effort while expanding CPET's applicability.

### [Bayesian Machine Learning Uncovers Hidden Risks in Schistosomiasis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-69528-4)
Schistosomiasis-induced hepatosplenomegaly has risk factors so complex they give epidemiologists headaches. This research uses Bayesian machine learning on multi-institutional cohort data, finding not just known risk factors but unexpected associations. More importantly, the model quantifies each factor's contribution for precision prevention. This "data-driven plus expert knowledge" combination could be a new paradigm for tackling complex diseases.

### [Explainable AI Makes Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplant Prognosis No Longer a Black Box](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02377-z)
Hematopoietic stem cell transplant prognosis prediction has always been a clinical puzzle. This research not only built a predictive model but used explainable AI to break down the model's decision logic—which factors matter most, how they interact, why it made this prediction. For doctors, this isn't a "trust me" black box but a "here's why" transparent tool. Explainability could be the key to winning doctor trust in medical AI.

### [Hybrid Ensemble Model Plus Attention Mechanism Advances Lung Cancer Detection](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-37187-6)
Lung cancer imaging AI is everywhere, but this research's strength is "explainability." It uses hybrid ensemble models plus attention fusion mechanisms to not only accurately classify lung cancer but highlight which regions the model focused on. Doctors see why AI made this judgment, not just blindly trusting a number. This "AI-assisted plus human decision-making" model might be more reliable than full automation.
