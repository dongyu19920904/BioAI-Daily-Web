## **🔥 Top 10 Today's Highlights**

### 1. Hearts That Look Fine: 42.9% Have Arterial Inflammation — All Missed by Blood Tests

Biograph, a preventive health company, [published an in-house study](https://longevity.technology/news/inflamed-heart-arteries-in-42-9-of-low-risk-adults/) on asymptomatic adults classified as "low risk" by conventional risk assessments. Using AI to analyze coronary artery CT scans (a standard cardiac imaging technique), they measured inflammation in the tissue surrounding the arteries. The results: 42.9% showed elevated inflammation, with 17.9% having extremely high inflammation around at least one major coronary artery. What's particularly striking is that roughly 47% of those with elevated inflammation showed zero plaque on CT. Cholesterol markers, blood glucose, general inflammation markers (hs-CRP, a blood test reflecting systemic inflammation), and the American Heart Association risk model all failed to flag these individuals. All participants were members of the same clinic, the total sample size wasn't disclosed, and the institution is both the researcher and the testing service provider. Peri-arterial inflammation is a warning signal — there's currently no evidence that intervening on it actually prevents heart attacks. 🫀

### 2. Less Than 42 Minutes of Sunlight a Day? Your Dementia Risk Association Is Stronger Than Six Known Factors

An observational study of over 87,000 adults found that more bright light exposure during the day was associated with a 16% lower dementia risk. Getting less than 42 minutes of bright daylight daily showed a stronger predictive association with dementia than six established risk factors, [according to Science Daily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261028232707.htm). This is a correlational study — it can't prove that sunlight itself prevents dementia. Specific sample composition, follow-up duration, and the publishing journal are still pending verification from the original source. ☀️

### 3. Artificial Sweeteners Linked to 62% Faster Cognitive Decline in Study of Nearly 13,000 People

A large analysis of nearly 13,000 adults found that those with the highest artificial sweetener intake experienced cognitive decline roughly 62% faster than those with the lowest intake — equivalent to an extra 1.6 years of aging — [according to Science Daily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261028234238.htm). All six common sweeteners studied were associated with accelerated cognitive decline. The researchers explicitly noted this is a correlation, not causation, and the journal source and detailed methods are pending verification from the original paper. 🧠

### 4. Monkey Study: Drug Combo Makes 93% of Weight Loss Come From Fat, Not Muscle

Semaglutide (a GLP-1 receptor agonist that curbs appetite by mimicking gut hormones) has faced criticism for causing significant muscle loss alongside fat loss. iBio tested its experimental antibody IBIO-610 in an 8-week non-human primate study: monkeys on semaglutide alone had 77% fat in their weight loss, but adding a single dose of IBIO-610 pushed that to 93%, [according to Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/ibios-primate-data-hint-at-muscle-sparing-obesity-add-on/). Just six animals per group over only 8 weeks — tiny sample size. The announcement also didn't compare total weight lost between groups. The first human safety trial is planned for the first half of 2027, and whether animal results will translate to humans remains to be seen. 💪

### 6. First Whole-Body Single-Cell Atlas of an Adult Vertebrate Built in a Tiny Transparent Fish

The Whitehead Institute sequenced over 600,000 cells from Danionella cerebrum — a transparent fish only about 12mm long — building the first complete whole-body single-cell atlas of an adult vertebrate, with the paper published in [Science](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/omics/first-whole-body-single-cell-atlas-of-adult-vertebrate-generated-in-danionella-cerebrum/). A single-cell transcriptomic atlas captures which genes each cell is actively using, essentially a "snapshot of work" happening across every cell in the organism. The atlas revealed how forebrain neurons sequentially go through immune response, stem cell activation, and new neuron maturation following injury. The dataset is publicly available on the interactive database DigiFish, providing a searchable reference for brain regeneration and aging research. The subject is fish — whether the mechanisms translate to human brains still needs targeted validation. 🐟

### 7. Why Do Stem Cell Therapies Work Inconsistently? In-Vitro Expansion May Be Triggering Cellular Senescence

Fight Aging! covered an open-access paper exploring a hidden quality problem in stem cell therapies: [cells may enter senescence en masse during in vitro expansion](https://www.fightaging.org/archives/2026/10/aspects-of-stem-cell-biology-that-impair-the-quality-of-stem-cell-therapies/) (senescent cells lose the ability to divide but keep pumping out pro-inflammatory signals), degrading the quality of what gets injected into patients. The paper specifically notes that when neural stem cells senesce, toxic proteins accumulate in their secretions, potentially triggering secondary senescence cascades in recipient cells. The authors recommend incorporating senescence level testing and extracellular vesicle protein composition analysis into stem cell production standards — though clinical feasibility and scalability still need further work. 🔬

### 8. FDA Clears Soft Mist Inhaler Trial Targeting COPD With Pulmonary Hypertension

United Therapeutics received FDA clearance to run a Phase II safety trial of the treprostinil soft mist inhaler in approximately 40 patients targeting pulmonary hypertension complicating COPD (PH-COPD) — a serious condition involving dangerously high blood pressure in the lungs — [according to Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/fda-clears-soft-mist-inhaler-trial-for-lung-disease/). The company estimates 400,000 to 800,000 Americans have both conditions simultaneously, but PH-COPD is chronically underdiagnosed because its symptoms overlap heavily with COPD itself, and there are currently no approved treatments. The 16-week double-blind trial's primary endpoint is safety and tolerability — not efficacy. If it goes well, Phase III is expected to kick off in 2028. 🫁

### 9. Klotho Protein Delivery: Cell Capsule Technology Validates Long-Term Secretion In Vitro

Avaí Bio announced a peer-reviewed publication in Frontiers in Aging reporting that their Klothonova platform — a joint venture with Austrianova — successfully achieved sustained Klotho protein production and secretion in vitro, [according to Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/avai-bio-reports-peer-reviewed-publication-on-sustained-klotho-delivery/). Klotho is a protein that declines with age and is closely linked to kidney, cardiovascular, and neurodegenerative diseases. The Cell-in-a-Box technology encapsulates living cells in porous capsules that allow nutrients and proteins to flow in and out while blocking immune system attacks. Results are currently in vitro only — optimal dosing, long-term safety, and clinical efficacy all need further research. 💊

### 10. Voyager Will Present Early Data on Two Tau-Targeting Therapies at Alzheimer's Conference

Voyager Therapeutics announced it will present two studies at the Clinical Trials on Alzheimer's Disease conference (CTAD 2026) running November 16–19, [according to Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/voyager-to-showcase-two-tau-therapies-at-ctad-2026/): VY1706, a gene therapy designed to reduce tau protein production in the brain (abnormal tau accumulation is a core pathology of Alzheimer's), will showcase its first-in-human study design; VY7523, a monoclonal antibody targeting a specific region of tau protein, will report baseline characteristics from a multiple ascending dose study. Both are in early exploratory stages — what's being presented is study design and baseline data, not efficacy results. 🧬

## **⚡ Product & Feature Updates**

### Fitnescity Health Opens API to Connect Health Platforms With 1,000+ Imaging Centers

Fitnescity Health [announced an open API integration](https://longevity.technology/news/health-apps-can-now-book-dexa-calcium-scans/) that lets any health platform plug into a single interface and offer users booking access across 1,000+ locations nationwide — including DEXA scans (using low-dose X-rays to estimate fat, muscle, and bone density ratios), coronary artery calcium CT scans (for cardiovascular risk assessment), and VO2 Max tests (measuring aerobic metabolic capacity). Ten platforms including Superpower are already live, letting users choose locations, book appointments, and view results all in-app. Scheduling compliance, physician oversight, and report retrieval are all handled by Fitnescity. The report doesn't cover scan costs or result interpretation standards — and whether routine imaging for asymptomatic individuals does more good than harm remains an open debate worth keeping in mind. 📱

### Diet and Heart Disease Risk in Cancer Survivors: Observational Study Published in JACC: Advances

A study published in the cardiology journal JACC: Advances found that among cancer survivors, healthier dietary habits were associated with lower cardiovascular disease incidence and mortality, [according to MedicalXpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-healthy-diet-heart-disease-cancer.html). Cancer survivors typically face elevated cardiovascular risk due to treatment side effects, making dietary intervention research in this population especially relevant. Specific participant count, follow-up duration, and dietary assessment methods are pending verification from the original source. As with all observational studies, other lifestyle factors can't be ruled out. 🥗

### Longevity Biotech Market Forecast to Top $31B by 2030 — But Extremely Fragmented

The Business Research Company [released a forecast](https://longevity.technology/news/longevity-biotech-market-set-to-top-31b-by-2030/) estimating the longevity biotech market will exceed $31 billion by 2030, with a compound annual growth rate of roughly 8%. Preventive health programs are expected to hold the largest share (about 41% of a broader $41B market), with biological age testing contributing roughly $5B. Notably, the report simultaneously flags that the market is extremely fragmented — the top 10 companies combined accounted for only about 0.3% of 2025 revenue, meaning no dominant player has emerged yet. Market forecasts are built on growth assumptions, not realized revenues — treat the numbers as trend indicators, not hard predictions. Clinical evidence for longevity biotech's efficacy continues to lag well behind the market hype. 📈
