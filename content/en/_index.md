## **🔥 Top 10 Stories**

### 1. [Growing Thymus in Spleens: Mouse Immune Systems "Turn Back the Clock"](https://lifespan.io/thymus-grafts-grow-in-mouse-spleens-restoring-immunity/)

Declining immunity in the elderly stems partly from thymic involution—this organ trains T cells to recognize pathogens. A Chinese research team had an idea: transplant neonatal mouse thymus tissue into spleens. The spleen's abundant blood supply and high immune tolerance allowed thymus fragments to grow into functional organs there, reaching half the volume of a normal thymus. Eight weeks post-transplant, 20-month-old aged mice (equivalent to 60-year-old humans) showed restored circulating T cell counts, virus-clearing ability approaching young mice after infection, and significantly inhibited melanoma growth.

**Caveats**: This is a mouse animal study using allogeneic tissue, requiring solutions for rejection responses and sourcing human thymus tissue. CD4+ T cell recovery only reached 50% of normal levels. Long-term safety and human applicability remain unknown.

**Source Type**: Peer-reviewed paper (Advanced Science) / Animal experiment (mouse in vivo, humanized mouse model) / Credibility: Medium-high

## **📎 Today's Citable Points**

### Point 1: Intraspleen thymus transplantation can partially reverse immune aging in aged mice
- **Factual Conclusion**: After transplanting neonatal mouse thymus tissue into the spleens of 20-month-old aged mice, Chinese researchers observed that grafts formed functional thymic tissue, circulating T cell counts recovered, and virus-clearing and tumor-suppressing capabilities approached those of young control mice.
- **Original Source**: [Thymus Grafts Grow in Mouse Spleens, Restoring Immunity](https://lifespan.io/thymus-grafts-grow-in-mouse-spleens-restoring-immunity/) (published in Advanced Science)
- **Evidence Boundaries**: Limited to mouse animal experiments (C57BL/6J and BALB/c strains, humanized mouse models). CD4+ T cell recovery reached only 50% of normal levels. Human safety, allogeneic transplant rejection risks, and ethical/accessibility concerns regarding thymus tissue sourcing remain unverified.

### Point 2: Spleen microenvironment surpasses muscle in supporting thymus tissue reconstruction
- **Factual Conclusion**: Compared to the traditional thigh muscle transplant site, intraspleen thymus grafts grew faster and developed more complete tissue architecture (forming cortical and medullary zones within 2 weeks) with less necrosis.
- **Original Source**: [Thymus Grafts Grow in Mouse Spleens, Restoring Immunity](https://lifespan.io/thymus-grafts-grow-in-mouse-spleens-restoring-immunity/) (published in Advanced Science)
- **Evidence Boundaries**: The study compared spleen versus muscle transplant sites but did not clarify the relative contributions of individual factors (blood supply, growth factors, progenitor cells) nor test other potential transplant sites.
