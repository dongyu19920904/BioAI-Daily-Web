### 1. Brain Serotonin Signaling Ramps Up With Age, Suppressing the Drive to Move in Aging Mice

A University of Aberdeen study published in *Metabolism* found that serotonin neurons in the dorsal raphe nucleus of aging mice fire more frequently during the animals' active periods. This activates inhibitory interneurons and reduces dopamine release from the ventral tegmental area — dopamine being the key neurotransmitter that drives motivation to move. After researchers used a virus to selectively eliminate these serotonin neurons, mice aged 14–20 months showed improved activity levels, movement speed, and grip strength; body fat also dropped over eight weeks with no change in food intake. According to [a report by Lifespan.io](https://lifespan.io/serotonin-signals-suppress-physical-activity-in-aging-mice/), the team first confirmed the pattern of declining activity in older adults using accelerometers on Scottish volunteers aged 17–70, validating the mouse model. These results are still limited to animals — targeted neuron elimination isn't applicable to humans, so future research will need to find gentler ways to intervene. 🧠

### 2. AI-Estimated "Cardiac Age" Being Older Than Actual Age Linked to Worse Health Outcomes

Research presented at ESC Digital & AI Summit 2026 showed that AI analysis of echocardiograms can estimate a person's "cardiac age," and when that estimate exceeds their actual age, it correlates with worse health outcomes. "Cardiac age" is a composite metric of cardiac function — think of it like biological age, but specifically for the heart, measuring how much the heart's aging outpaces the calendar. According to [a report by News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261113/Older-AI-estimated-heart-age-linked-to-worse-health-outcomes.aspx), this remains an associational finding; specific sample sizes and follow-up durations are pending verification, and it cannot yet be said that the AI estimate itself can predict or change an individual's cardiac disease risk. ❤️

### 3. A Molecule Produced by Gut Probiotics Can Rapidly Repair Intestinal Lining Damage

Scientists at Rutgers University's Institute for Health discovered that a molecule produced by "good" gut bacteria can rapidly repair damaged intestinal mucosa and promote the production of a coenzyme that naturally declines with age. The intestinal mucosa is the barrier that keeps bacteria and toxins out of the bloodstream; when it's damaged, it's associated with serious conditions like inflammatory bowel disease. According to [a report by News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261113/Researchers-discover-bacterial-molecule-that-rapidly-repairs-intestinal-damage.aspx), the molecule's specific identity, experimental model (cell, animal, or human), and detailed data are still pending verification. What we can confirm is that the research sits at the intersection of gut microbiota, intestinal repair, and aging-related metabolism. 🦠

### 4. Merck Launches Phase 3 Trial to Test Whether COPD Maintenance Drug Can Reduce Acute Flare-Ups

Ohtuvayre (ensifentrine), a COPD drug, received FDA approval in June 2024 for adult maintenance treatment — but the original approval trials didn't specifically measure whether it could reduce "acute exacerbations," the sudden worsening episodes that make COPD so debilitating. Merck has now launched a Phase 3 clinical trial called ENHANCE-3, planning to enroll roughly 2,136 patients across the US and other sites with one year of follow-up, directly targeting that question. According to [a report by Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/merck-tests-a-copd-drug-against-flare-ups/), the first patients have started enrolling — though enrollment isn't the same as completing treatment, and results won't be available until the trial wraps up. 💊

### 5. NKGen Biotech Presents 12-Month Natural Killer Cell Therapy Data for a Single Alzheimer's Patient

NKGen Biotech presented a poster at the CTAD conference in Boston covering just one Alzheimer's patient, documenting 12 consecutive months of observation under "natural killer cell therapy" — a treatment approach that uses immune cells to attack pathological targets. According to [a report by Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/one-alzheimers-patient-12-more-months-of-therapy/), a single-case report can't be compared against a control group, so no conclusions about efficacy can be drawn. Specific outcome data are pending verification, but these kinds of early observations typically serve as safety and tolerability references for larger trials down the line. 🔬

## **◎ Industry Moves & What They Mean for You**

### Evommune Licenses New Autoimmune Drug EVO570 for $30M Upfront, Phase 1 Trial on the Way

Evommune announced on November 4th that it licensed the oral drug EVO570 from Chengdu-based biotech Hinova Pharmaceuticals for $30 million upfront. EVO570 is a degrader targeting the VAV1 protein, intended to treat autoimmune diseases. VAV1 is a key signaling molecule in immune cell activation, and degrading it aims to suppress an overactive immune response. According to [a report by Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/evommune-bets-30m-upfront-on-a-new-autoimmune-drug/), milestone payments could total up to roughly $1.1 billion, and a Phase 1 human safety trial is about to kick off — though Phase 1 only evaluates safety and can't yet speak to clinical benefit for patients. 💰

## **❓ Related Questions**

### If my "cardiac age" is older than my actual age, does that mean my heart has already aged ahead of schedule?

Not so fast. The AI-estimated "cardiac age" is a statistical metric based on features in ultrasound images, and it reflects associations at the population level. A single elevated reading correlates with worse health outcomes, but that doesn't mean any particular person's heart has definitively aged beyond its years or developed disease. According to [a report by News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261113/Older-AI-estimated-heart-age-linked-to-worse-health-outcomes.aspx), what was released is an associational finding — the specific predictive power and clinical usage standards still need more research to nail down. 🤔
