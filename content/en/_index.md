## **🔥 Top 10 Daily Highlights**

### 1. Parkinson's Cell Therapy: One Shot Adds Five Hours of "Good Time" Per Day

XellSmart's XS411 is a dopaminergic neuron derived from induced pluripotent stem cells (ordinary cells reprogrammed to a pluripotent state and then guided into a specific cell type), surgically injected deep into the brain's motor regions. In a Phase 1 trial of 9 Parkinson's patients with an average disease duration of roughly a decade, a single transplant at the six-month mark gave patients on average more than five extra hours per day of controlled symptoms with no abnormal involuntary movements, with motor scores improving by an average of more than 30 points — every participant cleared the accepted threshold for clinically meaningful change. ¹⁸F-DOPA PET scans showed elevated dopamine synthesis activity at the injection site, supporting the inference that the cells are surviving. The therapy's edge here is that it sidesteps donor-recipient immune matching in favor of a standardized immunosuppression protocol, which — if it holds — dramatically lowers the bar for scale-up. A 30-person Phase 2 trial has completed enrollment and uses blinded outcome assessment; the control group data is the real test. The nine-person sample can't tell us anything about dose-response, and the company's self-reported data still awaits independent verification. Check out [Longevity.Technology's coverage](https://longevity.technology/news/xellsmarts-parkinsons-cell-shot-adds-5-good-hours-a-day/) for trial design details and imaging results. 🧠

### 2. Epigenetic Reprogramming's First Human Trial: Glaucoma Patients Show Visual Field Improvement Signals

Life Biosciences has released early Phase 1 human data for its glaucoma drug ER-100. ER-100 uses a single intravitreal injection to deliver three transcription factors (OCT4, SOX2, KLF4 — collectively called OSK) into retinal ganglion cells, then pairs that with 56 days of oral doxycycline to activate expression. The goal is to "reset" damaged cells to a younger gene expression state — that's the epigenetic repair concept in a nutshell. All 3 open-angle glaucoma patients tolerated the treatment well, with no dose-limiting toxicity or serious adverse events. Two of them showed visual field improvement signals at day 56, with more than 28% of test points improving by over 4 dB in each case. The Data Safety Monitoring Board has recommended advancing to the second dose cohort. A Phase 1 design only evaluates safety and tolerability — 3 patients, no controls, so the visual field improvements are early observational signals, not efficacy confirmation. The full trial protocol is at [clinicaltrials.gov (NCT07290244)](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07290244), and [Lifespan.io's coverage](https://lifespan.io/life-biosciences-announces-first-in-human-phase-1-trial-data/) has the detailed data breakdown. 👁️

### 3. Can a One-Time Gut Procedure Lock In Weight Loss After Stopping GLP-1s?

Fractyl Health's Revita is an endoscopic ablation procedure targeting the duodenum (the very first section of the small intestine), designed to reset the nutrient-sensing function of the gut lining. In the pivotal REMAIN-1 trial of 308 obese adults — all of whom had stopped the weight-loss drug tirzepatide and were regaining weight — the Revita group rebounded an average of 5.1% body weight at six months, versus 8.2% in the sham procedure group (p=0.0023). Revita patients held onto roughly 80% of their drug-achieved weight loss, compared to 67% for the sham group. That said, the absolute difference is only about three percentage points, and that "53% reduction in regain" figure comes from a post-hoc analysis of patients with larger ablation areas — exploratory territory. The real milestone is the 12-month data: the FDA requires that more than half of Revita patients remain at least 5% below their pre-drug weight, with results expected in Q1 2027. Revita is still an investigational device in the US. ### 4. NAD+ Pathway Activator Paired with a GLP-1: Phase 2 Trial Hits 50% Enrollment

Sironax is testing SIR4156, an oral small molecule that directly activates the NAMPT enzyme — the key enzyme in the cell's own NAD+ synthesis pathway. NAD+ is the molecule that regulates energy metabolism and muscle function, and it declines with age. Unlike supplementing NAD+ precursors like NMN, SIR4156 hits the synthesis pathway directly, making it a first-in-class approach. The combo with semaglutide (a GLP-1 weight-loss drug) is aimed at preserving muscle mass and improving body composition during weight loss. The global Phase 2 FORGE-LM trial, a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled design, has hit roughly 50% enrollment. The supporting evidence so far comes from mouse studies — no human results yet — and muscle mass is a secondary endpoint, not primary. FDA approval is still a long way off. ### 5. AI Drug Developer Insilico's Mesothelioma Drug Shows Response Signals in Phase 1

Insilico Medicine presented first-in-human Phase 1 data for ISM6331 at the 2026 European Society for Medical Oncology congress. Among 36 enrolled patients (median of 2 prior lines of therapy), the 130 mg once-daily cohort had 2 objective responses out of 5 evaluable patients (tumor shrinkage of 58% and 36%), hitting a 40% objective response rate in that cohort. Across the full dose range (30–130 mg), 22 evaluable patients showed an 18.2% overall response rate and a 68.2% disease control rate. Three of the 4 responding patients showed a response at their first scan at week 8. No dose-limiting toxicities were observed in the 15–130 mg range, and grade 3 adverse events came in at 8.3%. Mesothelioma is a notoriously hard-to-treat, aging-associated cancer, and this drug's target and molecule were AI-assisted. This is a small Phase 1 safety exploration with no control arm — a Phase 2 dose-optimization study is next. ### 6. Cell Therapy for Infants with Congenital Heart Disease: Phase 2 Shows Significantly Fewer Major Cardiac Events

Longeveron has released updated Phase 2 data for Laromestrocel in the ELPIS II randomized trial of 40 infants with hypoplastic left heart syndrome — a congenital defect where the left ventricle is nearly absent at birth. The trial missed its primary endpoint (change in right ventricular ejection fraction at 12 months), but the pre-specified secondary endpoint of "major adverse cardiac events" hit statistical significance: 8 events in the standard-of-care group versus 1 in the Laromestrocel group (p=0.043). The drug was developed in partnership with the NIH's National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, and there were no treatment-related serious adverse events at 12 months. Missing the primary endpoint is the core limitation, and the secondary endpoint result still needs independent validation. The company plans to meet with the FDA to discuss next steps. Full data will be presented as a late-breaking oral presentation at the American Heart Association annual meeting on November 8 — follow [Longevity.Technology's coverage](https://longevity.technology/news/updated-phase-2b-data-show-fewer-major-cardiac-events-in-hlhs/) to stay in the loop. ❤️

### 7. Vitamin B5 Derivative Pantethine Modulates Immune Cells, Improves Psoriasis Symptoms in Small Trial

A study published in the *Journal of Experimental Medicine* found that dendritic cells — key immune coordinators of inflammatory responses — show abnormally elevated Vanin enzyme activity in psoriasis patients. This lowers their own pantethine levels (pantethine is a vitamin B5 derivative and an over-the-counter cholesterol-lowering supplement), which in turn promotes cholesterol buildup and over-activates immune responses. The researchers confirmed in mice that pantethine supplementation suppresses inflammation, then ran a small controlled trial in 24 psoriasis patients. Among the 12 who added oral pantethine on top of standard topical treatment, 12-week symptom improvement was better than the 12-person control group using topical treatment only, with greater benefit in patients with elevated cholesterol. The researchers themselves describe this as proof-of-concept — 24 patients isn't enough to confirm efficacy, and the mechanistic causal chain still needs larger-scale validation. ### 8. Swiss Pharma Giant Ferring Moves to Acquire Ovarian Cell Engineering Company Gameto for Up to $1.38B

Ferring Pharmaceuticals has signed an agreement to acquire US biotech Gameto in a deal valued at up to $1.3825 billion, with the majority tied to R&D and commercial milestones — the final number gets confirmed as conditions are met. Gameto's core technology is stem cell-engineered ovarian support cells that help immature eggs complete development in vitro. ### 9. First Human Vascular Aging Cell Atlas Is Here

The Human Aging Endothelial Cell Atlas has been created by an international collaboration led by Laura de Rooij's team at the CeMM Research Center for Molecular Medicine of the Austrian Academy of Sciences. Endothelial cells line the inner walls of blood vessels throughout the body — and until now, nobody had systematically mapped how they change across a human lifetime. This is the first comprehensive reference atlas in the field, providing molecular-level foundational data for understanding vascular aging mechanisms. ### 10. mTORC1-Modulating Skincare Serum Improves Skin Elasticity in Double-Blind Trial of 47 Women

Rapalogix has published randomized double-blind controlled trial results for its Re-Q anti-aging serum (active ingredient RLX-201) in the *Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology*. mTORC1 is a nutrient-sensing cellular pathway tied to the regulation of aging hallmarks — rapamycin extends lifespan by inhibiting this same pathway. RLX-201 is designed to selectively modulate that pathway's activity in skin. Forty-seven women aged 35–65 used the product for 12 weeks: 31 got the real thing, 16 got an inactive vehicle control. At week 12, investigator-assessed skin firmness and wrinkle improvement was roughly 21%, with elasticity measured by elastometer peaking at week 8 and holding through week 12. Key caveats: all percentages are compared to each individual's own baseline, the report doesn't give the control group's specific change values, three co-founders are on the author list, the sample size is small, and this company-sponsored study alone isn't enough to make a recommendation. ## **🧪 Frontier Research**

### How Gut Dysbiosis May Accelerate Osteoporosis Through the Bone Marrow Immune Microenvironment

A review paper has mapped out the research connecting gut microbiome aging to osteoporosis. As we age, the gut microbiome shifts toward more pro-inflammatory strains and fewer strains that produce beneficial metabolites — that's dysbiosis. The resulting inflammatory signals may leak through the gut barrier into the bloodstream, reach the bone marrow, and tip the balance between osteoclasts (bone matrix breakdown) and osteoblasts (bone rebuilding) in favor of the former, driving bone mass loss. The paper also discusses the potential role of neural pathways — specifically the vagus nerve and sympathetic nervous system — in transmitting these signals. This is an observational mechanistic review: the causal contributions of each pathway aren't yet systematically established, and there are no direct clinical intervention takeaways. Follow [Fight Aging!'s summary](https://www.fightaging.org/archives/2026/10/reviewing-what-is-known-of-how-the-aging-gut-microbiome-may-contribute-to-osteoporosis/) to trace back to the original paper. 🦴

## **⌘ Top Open Source Projects**

### limitlessprojects/ClockBench: A Neutral Benchmarking Testbed for Epigenetic Aging Clocks

[ClockBench](https://github.com/limitlessprojects/ClockBench) is a benchmarking tool for researchers who want to cross-compare different epigenetic aging clocks — algorithms that estimate biological age from DNA methylation patterns — across different software implementations and datasets. The tool's core focus is "leakage-aware" testing, meaning it checks for information overlap between a clock's training data and test data, which is a common reliability pitfall in biological age research. A solid pick for bioinformatics researchers who need to compare or validate multiple aging clock outputs. The repo describes itself as a "continuously updated neutral benchmark" — check the README for supported clocks and input formats. Currently sitting at 1 star.

### hbc/app\_mavrikaki\_brain\_aging\_spatialtx\_human\_hbc05086: Human Brain Aging Spatial Transcriptomics Data Visualization

[This repo](https://github.com/hbc/app_mavrikaki_brain_aging_spatialtx_human_hbc05086) is built on the Vitessce framework for interactively browsing a human spatial transcriptomics dataset related to brain aging. Spatial transcriptomics measures gene expression while preserving tissue location information, letting researchers see exactly where in the brain aging-related gene expression changes are happening. Aimed at researchers with spatial genomics data analysis experience. The repo description is minimal — check the repo page for data contents and usage terms. Currently at 1 star.

## **❓ Related Questions**

### Does Life Biosciences' Visual Field Improvement Mean ER-100 Works for Glaucoma?

Not something we can conclude from this. This was an open-label Phase 1 trial with three participants, designed solely to evaluate safety and tolerability — no control group. The visual field improvements in two patients are early observational signals. Positive signals in a Phase 1 trial justify moving to a higher dose cohort, but confirming efficacy requires a larger trial with a control group.
