### 1. XellSmart's XS411: One Cell Shot Adds Five "Normal Hours" a Day for Parkinson's Patients

XS411, XellSmart's experimental cell therapy, is a dopamine neuron product made from induced pluripotent stem cells (ordinary cells reset to a pluripotent state and then redirected to become neurons), surgically injected deep into the brain's motor regions. In a phase 1 trial of 9 Parkinson's patients averaging about 10 years since diagnosis, a single transplant gave patients an average of over 5 extra hours per day of symptom-controlled time without abnormal involuntary movements, motor scores improved by an average of more than 30 points, and all participants crossed the widely accepted threshold for clinically meaningful change. ¹⁸F-DOPA PET scans showed elevated dopamine synthesis activity at the injection site, supporting the interpretation that the cells survived. A key advantage: no donor-recipient immune matching is needed — the therapy uses a standardized immunosuppression regimen, which if maintained would dramatically lower the bar for scaled production. A phase 2 trial of 30 patients has completed enrollment with blinded outcome assessment — the control group data will be the real test; nine patients can't tell us anything about dose-response, and the company's self-reported data still awaits independent verification. Check out the [Longevity.Technology report](https://longevity.technology/news/xellsmarts-parkinsons-cell-shot-adds-5-good-hours-a-day/) for trial design and imaging result details.

### 2. Life Biosciences' ER-100: First-in-Human Epigenetic Reprogramming Shows Visual Field Improvement in Glaucoma Patients

ER-100, Life Biosciences' glaucoma drug, delivered its first-in-human phase 1 early data. The drug works via a single intravitreal injection that introduces three transcription factors (OCT4, SOX2, KLF4 — collectively OSK) into retinal ganglion cells, paired with a 56-day course of oral doxycycline to activate expression. The goal: "reset" damaged cells to a younger gene expression state — i.e., epigenetic repair. All 3 open-angle glaucoma patients tolerated the treatment well, with no dose-limiting toxicity or serious adverse events; 2 of them showed visual field improvement signals at day 56, with over 28% of tested points per person improving by more than 4 dB. The data safety monitoring board recommended escalation to the second dose cohort. The phase 1 design only assesses safety and tolerability — 3 patients, no control group, and visual field improvement is a preliminary observational signal that can't confirm efficacy. Check [clinicaltrials.gov (NCT07290244)](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07290244) for the full trial protocol, and the [Lifespan.io report](https://lifespan.io/life-biosciences-announces-first-in-human-phase-1-trial-data/) for detailed data presentation.

### 3. Fractyl Health's Revita: Can a One-Time Gut Procedure Hold the Line After Stopping Weight-Loss Injections?

Revita, Fractyl Health's endoscopic ablation device targeting the duodenum (the very first segment of the small intestine), aims to reset the nutrient-sensing function of the intestinal lining. In the pivotal REMAIN-1 trial of 308 obese adults, all participants had stopped the weight-loss drug tirzepatide and regained weight before receiving Revita — at six months, the Revita group rebounded an average of 5.1% body weight vs. 8.2% for the sham group (p=0.0023), meaning Revita preserved about 80% of the drug-induced weight loss compared to 67% for sham. That ~3 percentage point absolute difference isn't huge, though, and the 53% rebound reduction figure comes from a post-hoc analysis of larger ablation areas — an exploratory conclusion. The real make-or-break moment is the 12-month data: the FDA requires that more than half of Revita patients still weigh at least 5% less than their pre-drug baseline, with results expected in Q1 2027. Revita remains an investigational device in the US. See the [Longevity.Technology report](https://longevity.technology/news/weight-regain-after-glp-1s-a-gut-procedures-test/) for trial design and subgroup data.

### 4. Sironax's SIR4156: NAD+ Pathway Activator Teams Up With Weight-Loss Injections, Phase 2 Half-Enrolled

SIR4156, Sironax's oral small-molecule drug, activates the NAMPT enzyme — the key enzyme cells use to make their own NAD+, a molecule that regulates energy metabolism and muscle function and declines with age. Unlike supplementing NAD+ precursors (like NMN), SIR4156 directly switches on this synthesis pathway, making it first-in-class. It's being combined with semaglutide (a fellow weight-loss injection) with the main goal of reducing muscle loss and improving body composition during weight loss. The global phase 2 FORGE-LM trial has enrolled about 50% of its target, using a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design. The supporting evidence so far comes from mouse studies — no human results have been released yet, and muscle mass is a secondary endpoint, not the primary one. The FDA has not approved this drug. Check out the [Longevity.Technology report](https://longevity.technology/news/can-an-nad-booster-protect-muscle-on-semaglutide/) for more on the trial design and NAD+ pathway background.

### 5. Insilico Medicine's ISM6331: AI-Designed Mesothelioma Drug Shows Response Signals in Phase 1

ISM6331, Insilico Medicine's AI-designed drug for mesothelioma, presented its first-in-human phase 1 data at the 2026 European Oncology Congress. Among 36 enrolled patients (median 2 prior lines of therapy), the 130 mg once-daily cohort had 2 objective responses out of 5 evaluable patients (tumor shrinkage of 58% and 36%), giving the 130 mg group a 40% objective response rate. Across all dose levels (30–130 mg), 22 evaluable patients showed an overall response rate of 18.2% and a disease control rate of 68.2%. Of the 4 responding patients, 3 showed response at their very first scan at week 8. No dose-limiting toxicity was observed across the 15–130 mg range, with grade 3 adverse events at 8.3%. Mesothelioma is a treatment-resistant, aging-associated cancer, and this drug had both its target and molecule designed with AI assistance. This is a small-sample phase 1 safety exploration with no control group — a phase 2 dose optimization study is planned next. See the [Longevity.Technology report](https://longevity.technology/news/insilico-reports-positive-early-human-trial-results-for-mesothelioma-drug/) for detailed dose data.

### 6. Longeveron's Laromestrocel: Phase 2 in Congenital Heart Defect Babies Shows Major Cardiac Events Significantly Reduced

Laromestrocel, Longeveron's cell therapy, showed updated ELPIS II phase 2 randomized trial data in 40 infants with hypoplastic left heart syndrome — a congenital defect where the left ventricle is nearly absent at birth. The trial missed its primary endpoint (change in right ventricular ejection fraction at 12 months), but the pre-specified secondary endpoint of "major adverse cardiac events" hit statistical significance: 8 events in the standard treatment group vs. 1 in the Laromestrocel group (p=0.043). The drug was developed in collaboration with the NIH's National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, with no treatment-related serious adverse events over 12 months. Missing the primary endpoint is the core limitation, and the secondary endpoint results still need independent validation; the company plans to meet with the FDA to discuss next steps. Full data will be presented as a late-breaking featured oral presentation at the American Heart Association's annual meeting on November 8 — follow along via the [Longevity.Technology report](https://longevity.technology/news/updated-phase-2b-data-show-fewer-major-cardiac-events-in-hlhs/) for updates.

### 7. Pantethine (Vitamin B5 Derivative) Tames Immune Cells, Small Trial Improves Psoriasis Symptoms

Pantethine — a derivative of vitamin B5 and a commercially available cholesterol-lowering supplement — is the star of a study published in the *Journal of Experimental Medicine*. Dendritic cells (key immune system coordinators of inflammatory responses) in psoriasis patients show abnormally elevated Vanin enzyme activity, which depletes their own pantethine levels, promoting cholesterol buildup and overactivating immune responses. Researchers demonstrated in mice that pantethine supplementation can suppress inflammation, and in a small controlled trial of 24 psoriasis patients, the 12 who added oral pantethine on top of standard topical treatment showed better symptom improvement at 12 weeks than the 12 who used only topical treatment — with bigger benefits in those with higher cholesterol. The researchers themselves call this a proof-of-concept stage: 24 patients isn't enough to confirm efficacy, and the mechanistic causal link still needs larger-scale validation. Check out the [GEN report](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/drug-discovery/vitamin-derivative-pantethine-alleviates-psoriasis-symptoms-in-small-human-trial/) for the original links and trial data.

### 9. First Human Vascular Aging Cell Atlas Completed

The Human Aging Endothelial Cell Atlas — the first comprehensive reference map of how endothelial cells (the cells lining every blood vessel in the body) change across a human lifespan — has been created by an international collaboration led by Laura de Rooij's team at the CeMM Research Center for Molecular Medicine of the Austrian Academy of Sciences. No one had ever systematically mapped how these cells change with age before. This atlas provides molecular-level baseline data for understanding vascular aging mechanisms. ### 10. Rapalogix's Re-Q Serum: mTORC1-Modulating Skincare Improves Skin Elasticity in 47-Woman Double-Blind Trial

Re-Q, Rapalogix's anti-aging serum with active ingredient RLX-201, published its randomized double-blind controlled trial results in the *Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology*. mTORC1 is a nutrient-sensing cellular pathway linked to aging hallmark regulation — rapamycin also produces its life-extending effects by inhibiting this pathway; RLX-201 is designed to selectively modulate this pathway's activity in skin. 47 women aged 35–65 used the product for 12 weeks — 31 with the real deal, 16 with an inactive vehicle control. At week 12, investigator-assessed skin firmness and wrinkle improvement came in around 21%, and elastometry showed elasticity peaking at week 8 and holding through week 12. Key limitations: all percentages are compared against each person's own baseline, the report doesn't give specific change values for the control group, three co-founders are among the paper's authors, and the sample is small — this one company-led study isn't enough to make a recommendation. See the [Longevity.Technology report](https://longevity.technology/news/rapalogix-tests-longevity-face-serum-on-47-women/) for trial methods and skin biopsy data.

## **🔬 Frontier Research**

### Gut Microbiome Imbalance Accelerates Osteoporosis Through Bone Marrow Immune Microenvironment

Gut microbiome aging and its connection to osteoporosis — that's what a review paper sets out to map. As we age, pro-inflammatory bacterial strains increase while beneficial metabolite-producing strains decrease in the gut microbiome (i.e., dysbiosis). The resulting inflammatory signals may leak through the intestinal barrier into the bloodstream, reach bone marrow, and disrupt the balance between osteoclasts (which break down bone matrix) and osteoblasts (which rebuild it) — aging tips this balance toward the former, causing bone loss. The paper also discusses potential roles of neural pathways (the vagus nerve and sympathetic nervous system) in transmitting these signals. This is an observational mechanistic review — it hasn't systematically established the causal contribution of each pathway, and there are no clinically actionable intervention conclusions. Interested readers can trace the original paper via the [Fight Aging! summary](https://www.fightaging.org/archives/2026/10/reviewing-what-is-known-of-how-the-aging-gut-microbiome-may-contribute-to-osteoporosis/).

## **💻 Open Source Top Projects**

### limitlessprojects/ClockBench: A Neutral Benchmarking Testbed for Epigenetic Aging Clocks

[ClockBench](https://github.com/limitlessprojects/ClockBench) is a benchmarking tool for researchers to cross-compare different epigenetic aging clocks — algorithms that estimate biological age from DNA methylation patterns — across different software implementations and datasets to see if they produce consistent results. The tool's core focus is "leakage-aware" — detecting whether there's information overlap between training data and test data, a common issue that undermines the reliability of biological age studies. It's well-suited for bioinformaticians who need to compare or validate the outputs of multiple aging clocks. The repo describes itself as a "continuously updated neutral benchmark" — check the README for exactly which clocks and input formats are supported. Currently 1 star.

### hbc/app\_mavrikaki\_brain\_aging\_spatialtx\_human\_hbc05086: Human Brain Aging Spatial Transcriptome Data Visualization

[This repository](https://github.com/hbc/app_mavrikaki_brain_aging_spatialtx_human_hbc05086) is built on the Vitessce framework for interactively browsing human spatial transcriptome datasets related to brain aging. Spatial transcriptomics measures gene expression while preserving tissue location information, letting researchers see where aging-related gene changes occur in the brain. It's suited for researchers with experience in spatial genomics data analysis. The repo description is extremely brief — check the repo page for specific data content and usage conditions. Currently 1 star.

## **💡 Q&A**

### Does Life Biosciences' Visual Field Improvement Mean ER-100 Works for Glaucoma?

ER-100 can't be called effective based on this. This is a phase 1 open-label trial with three participants, designed only to assess safety and tolerability — no control group, and the visual field improvement seen in two patients is a preliminary observational signal. The positive signal in phase 1 justified moving to a higher dose cohort, but confirming efficacy requires a larger trial with a control group. The original trial registration is available on [clinicaltrials.gov (NCT07290244)](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07290244) for study design and inclusion/exclusion criteria, and the [Lifespan.io report](https://lifespan.io/life-biosciences-announces-first-in-human-phase-1-trial-data/) has the full background on the data presentation.
