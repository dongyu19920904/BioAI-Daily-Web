## Stay Away From These Today 🚫

**Content products based on occupational chemical exposure and Parkinson's disease associations**

Today's cross-sectional study (n=41,315, [Source material](<https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261118/From-paint-thinners-to-fuels-Could-workplace-chemicals-play-a-role-in-Parkinsone28099s-disease.aspx>)) found an association between industrial solvent exposure and Parkinson's risk, but the study itself explicitly notes limitations: restricted exposure assessment, age confounding, and self-reported diagnosis — causation cannot be established. This content type easily slides into "these chemicals cause Parkinson's" causal framing, which misleads general readers and creates unnecessary anxiety. If you do touch it, the only acceptable framing is "observational study found a correlation, cannot prove causation" — and there's no clear monetization path, so priority is low.

**MRI biomarker interpretation tools**

The study at [Source material](<https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.24487>) found that unmyelinated axons have a substantial effect on MRI diffusion and relaxation metrics, meaning existing MRI white matter microstructure biomarkers may carry systematic interpretation bias. The research stage is mouse models (cuprizone demyelination model), with no human validation yet. Any consumer-facing MRI report interpretation tool or content lacks clinical grounding at this evidence stage — not a viable product direction.

**The "early warning signs" content angle on the hypothalamus and Alzheimer's**

Buck Institute research found that the hypothalamus shows volume and gene activity changes before AD symptoms appear, involving disrupted sleep rhythms, metabolic changes, and stress hormone shifts ([Source material](<https://www.genengnews.com/topics/translational-medicine/alzheimers-disease-and-aging-linked-to-changes-in-the-hypothalamus/>)). This angle is easily retrofitted into "these symptoms warn of Alzheimer's" content, which constitutes unvalidated personal health judgment — that's firmly in compliance no-go territory. This research works as background material for a "early brain region changes in AD" explainer, but cannot be developed into any symptom checklist or self-assessment framework.
