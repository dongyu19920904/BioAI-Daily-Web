## **🔥 Top 10**

### 1. [Transplanted Immune Cells Donate Mitochondria to Neighbors, Improving Energy Metabolism](https://lifespan.io/transplanted-immune-cells-donate-mitochondria-to-neighbors/)
Mouse experiments show that after bone marrow transplant, healthy immune cells don't just replace diseased ones—they transfer mitochondria to surrounding neurons and cardiac cells. Recipients' mitochondrial respiration partially recovered, motor coordination improved, and cardiac pump function jumped ~50%. Recovery was incomplete, though: recipient cells performed somewhere between diseased and healthy. This was in a mouse model of Friedreich's ataxia, a rare disease. Human application needs further validation.

**Source Type**: Peer-reviewed paper (Nature Communications) / Evidence Stage: Animal study / Credibility: High

### 2. [Mitochondrial Health May Strongly Influence Learning Ability in Older Adults](https://lifespan.io/mitochondrial-performance-may-strongly-influence-learning/)
Researchers taught mice a rule, then flipped it. Aging mice's learning flexibility directly correlated with presynaptic mitochondrial function—not with conventional aging markers. After 20 weeks of MitoQ (a mitochondrial antioxidant), old mice adapted to new rules significantly faster. This suggests mitochondria-produced reactive oxygen species may damage neuronal structural plasticity. The study hasn't pinpointed specific intervention targets yet; clinical translation needs more work.

**Source Type**: Science news report / Evidence Stage: Animal study / Credibility: Medium

### 3. [Faster Biological Aging May Explain Rising Cancer Rates in Adults Under 55](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260913082200.htm)
Researchers found that recent generations are biologically older at the same chronological age. Accelerated biological aging correlated with higher early-onset cancer risk, especially lung, gastrointestinal, uterine, and colorectal cancers. The study didn't specify sample size, biological age measurement method, or causality. This is an observational association; no intervention target has been established.

**Source Type**: Science news report / Evidence Stage: Epidemiological observation / Credibility: Medium
