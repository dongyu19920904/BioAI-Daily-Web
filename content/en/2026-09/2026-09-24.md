## **🔥 Top 10 Stories**

### 1. [Discovery of Entirely New Anti-Aging Target: PTCHD4 Knockout Extends Mouse Lifespan by Months](https://lifespan.io/an-entirely-new-target-for-fighting-senescence/)

PTCHD4 is a protein that's been barely studied before. The research team found it's highly expressed in senescent cells, appearing in sync with the classic senescence marker p16. More importantly: mice with PTCHD4 knocked out lived several months longer than normal mice and didn't develop gray fur. In a lung fibrosis model, mice lacking PTCHD4 maintained better lung function with less immune infiltration. Mechanistically, PTCHD4 promotes senescence by activating the AKT signaling pathway—a known pathway, but PTCHD4 itself is a newly discovered upstream switch.

The research is still at the animal experiment stage. Whether PTCHD4 can become a reliable intervention target, whether it has side effects, and how to precisely inhibit it all need follow-up validation. Currently, we can only say it's strongly correlated with aging, not that it's the sole key factor for lifespan extension.

**Source Type**: Research news report / **Evidence Stage**: Animal experiments (mice) / **Credibility**: Medium

### 2. [Link Between Ultra-Processed Foods and Brain Aging: Scientific Progress and Cognitive Risk](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-09-science-ultraprocessed-foods-aging-brain.html)

Ultra-processed foods containing dyes, additives, and preservatives are now everywhere. Owen Carmichael at Louisiana State University's Pennington Biomedical Research Center is systematically studying their relationship with brain aging. There's already some consensus on metabolic health impacts, but long-term effects on cognitive function and neurodegenerative diseases remain unclear.

Research is ongoing with no specific data or conclusions published yet. The challenge with this type of epidemiological research is: it's hard to isolate the independent effect of ultra-processed foods from overall dietary patterns, lifestyle, and socioeconomic factors. Even if correlations are found, causal relationships can't be directly inferred.

**Source Type**: Research news report / **Evidence Stage**: Research in progress, no results published / **Credibility**: Low (no supporting data yet)

### 3. [AI Drug Company Basecamp Research Completes $140M Series C Funding with Anthropic and Nvidia Participating](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-nvidia-backed-basecamp-research-raise-140m-series-c-financing-toward-advancing-ai-designed-drugs/)

Basecamp uses its biological foundation model EDEN to design in vivo cell therapies—directly reprogramming cells inside patients rather than modifying them ex vivo and reinfusing like current CAR-T. EDEN's training data comes from the company's "trillion gene atlas," covering biodiversity samples from over 30 countries across seven continents. Preprints show EDEN-generated large serine recombinases have a 63.2% functional hit rate, with 50% active in human cells, achieving therapeutic-grade CAR insertion.

Anthropic participated through its $100M Anthology Fund, and Nvidia's venture arm NVentures also joined. Funding will be used to train the next generation EDEN model and advance preclinical research. Currently, there's only in vitro cell data and animal experiment support—human clinical trials are still distant. The safety, off-target effects, and long-term impacts of in vivo gene editing all need rigorous validation.

**Source Type**: Business news report / **Evidence Stage**: Funding announcement, preclinical research / **Credibility**: Medium (business progress clear, technical validation pending)

### 4. [Fruit Fly Study Reveals: Key Protein Lsp2 Regulates Lifespan Through mTORC1 in Dietary Restriction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11029-x)

Caloric restriction extends fruit fly lifespan, but the molecular mechanism has been unclear. This Nature-published study found that Lsp2 (lipid storage protein 2) is a key node connecting the mTORC1 signaling pathway and TOP mRNA translation. mTORC1 is a core switch regulating cell growth and metabolism, while TOP mRNA translation controls ribosomal protein synthesis. Research shows that during caloric restriction, Lsp2 expression increases, extending lifespan by suppressing mTORC1 activity and reducing protein synthesis.

This is a fruit fly experiment and can't be directly extrapolated to humans. However, the mTORC1 pathway is highly conserved in mammals. Whether Lsp2's human homolog has similar functions and can become an intervention target warrants follow-up research. Currently, we can only say it's part of the caloric restriction lifespan extension mechanism in fruit flies—this doesn't justify recommending human dietary intervention programs.

**Source Type**: Peer-reviewed paper (Nature) / **Evidence Stage**: Animal experiments (fruit flies) / **Credibility**: High (solid mechanistic research, but species extrapolation requires caution)

### 5. [Targeting BIRC6 Reverses Hematopoietic Stem Cell Aging and Immunosenescence](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41392-026-02787-1)

BIRC6 is an apoptosis inhibitor protein. Research found that inhibiting BIRC6 can restore aged hematopoietic stem cells to a youthful state and improve immune function. During aging, hematopoietic stem cells have reduced self-renewal capacity and skewed myeloid differentiation, leading to decreased immunity and increased inflammation. Targeting BIRC6 can partially reverse these changes.

Animal experiments show clear effects, but human trials haven't begun yet. Hematopoietic system interventions require extreme caution—ensuring normal hematopoiesis isn't damaged while avoiding potential tumor risk (apoptosis inhibition is closely related to cancer). Currently, we can't conclude that inhibiting BIRC6 can be safely used for human anti-aging.

**Source Type**: Peer-reviewed paper / **Evidence Stage**: Animal experiments, mechanistic research / **Credibility**: Medium (clear mechanism, clinical application pending validation)

### 6. [Role of Senescent Cells in Kidney Aging, Injury, and Fibrosis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41581-026-01123-8)

This is a review systematically examining the role of senescent cells in kidney disease. Senescent cells secrete pro-inflammatory factors, growth factors, and proteases (collectively called SASP), accelerating kidney fibrosis and functional decline. Animal experiments clearing senescent cells show they can reduce kidney damage and slow chronic kidney disease progression.

But a review doesn't equal clinical evidence. There are currently no senescent cell clearance therapies for kidney disease in clinical trials. Moreover, not all senescent cells are harmful—some play roles in tissue repair. How to precisely identify and selectively clear harmful senescent cells is an urgent problem to solve.

**Source Type**: Peer-reviewed review (Nature Reviews Nephrology) / **Evidence Stage**: Summary of animal experiments, no human trials / **Credibility**: Medium (high-quality review, but lacks human evidence)

### 7. [Earth's Magnetic Field May Affect Cellular Energy Production and Aging Process](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-09-earth-magnetic-field-age.html)

New research from the University of Nottingham suggests Earth's magnetic field may affect cellular energy production, exercise performance, and even lifespan. This is a surprising direction—conventionally, geomagnetic field strength is considered too weak to significantly affect biochemical processes. Researchers speculate it may work through the radical pair mechanism, a quantum effect sensitive to magnetic fields in certain chemical reactions.

This is only a preliminary observation with unclear mechanism and no specific data. Even if the geomagnetic field does have an effect, how significant the actual physiological meaning is and whether intervention methods can be developed are unknown. This type of research is better suited as a basic science lead rather than a basis for anti-aging programs.

### 8. [DNMT3A and TET2 Mutations Trigger Inflammation Through Different Pathways in Clonal Hematopoiesis](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-09-dark-genome-reveals-routes-inflammation.html)

Clonal hematopoiesis is an age-related phenomenon: mutated hematopoietic stem cells expand, forming numerous blood cells carrying the same mutation. DNMT3A and TET2 are the two most common mutated genes. GeroScience-published research found that while both mutations lead to inflammation, their mechanisms differ—they affect different parts of epigenetic regulation, activating different inflammatory pathways.

Understanding these differences helps develop precision intervention strategies. But whether clonal hematopoiesis itself needs intervention is still debated: most carriers remain asymptomatic throughout life, with only a small portion developing blood disorders. There are currently no approved therapies for clonal hematopoiesis, only regular monitoring.

**Source Type**: Peer-reviewed paper (GeroScience) / **Evidence Stage**: Mechanistic research, human cells and mouse models / **Credibility**: Medium (solid mechanistic research, clinical significance pending clarification)
