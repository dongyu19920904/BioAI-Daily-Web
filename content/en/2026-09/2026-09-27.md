## **🔥 Top 10 Stories**

### 1. [Growing Thymus in Spleens Turns Back the Clock on Mouse Immunity](https://lifespan.io/thymus-grafts-grow-in-mouse-spleens-restoring-immunity/)

Immune decline in the elderly stems largely from thymic involution—this organ trains T cells to recognize pathogens. A Chinese research team had an idea: transplant neonatal mouse thymus tissue into spleens. The spleen's abundant blood supply and high immune tolerance allowed thymus fragments to develop into functional organs, reaching half the size of a normal thymus. Eight weeks post-transplant, 20-month-old aged mice (roughly equivalent to 60-year-old humans) saw restored circulating T-cell counts, viral clearance approaching young-mouse levels after infection, and significantly inhibited melanoma growth.

**Conclusion boundaries**: This is a mouse animal study using allogeneic tissue transplants—rejection responses and human thymus tissue sourcing remain unresolved. CD4+ T-cell recovery only reached 50% of normal levels, with unknown long-term safety and human applicability.

**Source type**: Peer-reviewed paper (Advanced Science) / Animal experiment (in vivo mouse, humanized mouse model) / Credibility: Medium-high

## **📎 Today's Quotable Points**

### Point 1: Intralesional thymus transplantation partially reverses immune aging in aged mice
- **Factual conclusion**: A Chinese team transplanted neonatal mouse thymus tissue into the spleens of 20-month-old aged mice, forming functional thymus tissue that restored circulating T-cell counts and brought viral clearance and tumor suppression capabilities close to young-mouse controls.
- **Original source**: [Thymus Grafts Grow in Mouse Spleens, Restoring Immunity](https://lifespan.io/thymus-grafts-grow-in-mouse-spleens-restoring-immunity/) (published in Advanced Science)
- **Evidence boundaries**: Mouse animal experiments only (C57BL/6J and BALB/c strains, humanized mouse models), CD4+ T-cell recovery reached only 50% of normal levels, with unverified human safety, allogeneic transplant rejection risks, and ethical/accessibility concerns regarding thymus tissue sourcing.

### Point 2: The splenic microenvironment outperforms muscle in supporting thymus tissue reconstruction
- **Factual conclusion**: Compared to traditional thigh muscle transplant sites, intralesional thymus grafts grew faster, showed more complete tissue architecture (forming cortical and medullary zones within 2 weeks), and exhibited less necrosis.
- **Original source**: [Thymus Grafts Grow in Mouse Spleens, Restoring Immunity](https://lifespan.io/thymus-grafts-grow-in-mouse-spleens-restoring-immunity/) (published in Advanced Science)
- **Evidence boundaries**: The study compared spleen and muscle as two transplant sites but did not clarify the relative contributions of various factors (blood supply, growth factors, progenitor cells), nor test other potential transplant sites.
