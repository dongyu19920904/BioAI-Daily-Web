### 1. [Intermittent Fasting Shows Promise in Huntington's Disease Patient Trial](https://lifespan.io/intermittent-fasting-shows-promise-in-a-huntingtons-trial/)

Huntington's disease was previously considered untreatable. Oregon Health & Science University's trial had 20 early-stage patients eat only within a 6-8 hour window daily for 12 weeks. Results: composite disease score improved by 0.50 points (typically declines 1 point annually), plasma neurofilament light chain (neural damage marker) dropped an average of 12.6%, and peripheral blood mononuclear cell mitochondrial respiration and ATP production rose markedly. The study also found patients maintained weight, dispelling concerns that fasting might worsen weight loss. However, this is a small trial without a control group (20 participants), and subjects simultaneously received nutrition guidance and exercise advice—improvements could stem from multiple factors.

**Source type: Peer-reviewed paper (Nature Metabolism) / Early clinical trial (20 participants, no control) / Credibility: Medium**

### 2. [MIT Develops Noninvasive Method to Identify Aging "Zombie Cells" in the Body](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260921/MIT-researchers-develop-noninvasive-way-to-spot-aging-zombie-cells.aspx)

Senescent cells no longer divide but refuse to die; when they accumulate, they trigger cancer, tissue degeneration, and inflammation. MIT researchers have developed a noninvasive technique to locate these cells in the body. Previously, detection required biopsy or invasive methods; the new technique opens a window for monitoring senescent cell burden and evaluating clearance therapy effectiveness. However, the report doesn't disclose the technical principle, validation stage, or clinical feasibility—currently, this can only be confirmed as a research-stage tool.

**Source type: Media report / Research tool development (specific stage unknown) / Credibility: Medium**

### 3. [Mayo Clinic's Precure Selects Helix as Million-Scale Multi-Omics Partner](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/omics/precure-selects-helix-as-multiomics-lab-and-ai-tools-provider/)

Mayo Clinic launched the Precure initiative last week, aiming to generate molecular data from approximately 1 million biological samples and integrate genomic insights into clinical routine. Helix will provide clinical-grade whole genome sequencing (WGS), research-grade proteome sequencing, and AI tools, using Ultima Genomics' UG200 sequencing platform. This continues Mayo's Tapestry study (which has enrolled 100,000 participants), but the complete chain from sample collection to clinical application still requires years of validation. Helix joins as a minority stakeholder; Mayo's financial returns will be reinvested in research and education.

**Source type: Official release / Infrastructure development (data generation phase) / Credibility: High**

### 4. [Andelyn Biomanufacturing Platform Supports Fayuvi Gene Therapy Approval](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/omics/andelyn-curator-biomanufacturing-platform-supports-fayuvi-gene-therapy-approval/)

FDA approved Ultragenyx's Fayuvi gene therapy last week for treating a rare, fatal disease—Sanfilippo syndrome type A (MPS IIIA). This is the first AAV gene therapy manufactured using the Andelyn AAV Curator platform process to gain approval. MPS IIIA affects approximately 3,000-5,000 patients globally, with a median lifespan of 15 years; there were no previously approved therapies. Andelyn's facility in Columbus, Ohio has entered commercial production, with Ultragenyx's Bedford, Massachusetts plant producing in parallel. This marks further maturation of manufacturing capability and regulatory pathways for rare disease gene therapies.

**Source type: Official release / FDA-approved product (commercial) / Credibility: High**

### 5. [High Adolescent BMI Linked to Faster Biological Aging in Adulthood](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260921/High-adolescent-BMI-linked-to-faster-biological-aging-in-adulthood.aspx)

University of Jyväskylä in Finland research found that individuals with higher BMI during adolescence may age biologically faster than their chronological age in adulthood. The study, published in the International Journal of Obesity, tracked the relationship between adolescent BMI and adult epigenetic clocks (molecular markers measuring biological aging). This suggests adolescent weight management may impact long-term healthspan, but the study didn't specify sample size, follow-up duration, causal mechanisms, or whether confounding factors like diet and exercise were excluded.

**Source type: Peer-reviewed paper / Observational study / Credibility: Medium**
