## **🔥 Today's Top 10 Focus**

### 1. Alzheimer's immunotherapy sees biomarker improvements in first human trial

According to [media reports](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/translational-medicine/beyond-amyloid-and-tau-alzheimers-drug-developer-sees-early-clinical-success-for-immunotherapy-based-approach/), ImmunoBrain's antibody IBC-Ab002 showed safety in a Phase I trial of 40 early Alzheimer's patients and demonstrated drops in cerebrospinal fluid markers linked to neuronal damage at 48 weeks. The drug doesn't directly clear amyloid but instead briefly blocks the PD-1/PD-L1 immune "brake" pathway, mobilizing peripheral immune cells into the brain to ease inflammation; the 30 mg/kg group saw neuronal granin drop about 23% with no brain hemorrhages or ARIA. Data published in Nature Medicine, but small sample with no statistical significance makes this an exploratory signal—the team has submitted for Phase II, and whether it changes disease course awaits verification.

### 2. Partial reprogramming outcomes depend on cell type

According to [Lifespan.io reporting](https://lifespan.io/partial-reprogrammings-outcome-depends-on-cell-type/), a preprint involving reprogramming pioneer Shinya Yamanaka tested "partial reprogramming" on three human cell types—skin fibroblasts, killer T cells, and aortic endothelial cells—by briefly activating the OSKM four genes to rejuvenate cells without fully reverting them to stem cells. The three cell types reacted very differently: endothelial cells dropped in epigenetic age while function improved; T cells after deep reprogramming couldn't recover and lost identity; fibroblasts dropped in epigenetic age but suffered long-term proliferation damage. This is a preprint not yet peer-reviewed, using only in vitro cells from 48-63 year-old donors, and can't extrapolate to human organs.

### 3. Brain scan privacy-preserving collaborative framework classifies neurological diseases across sites

According to [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.21955), the SCAN framework lets multiple hospitals collaboratively adapt a 3D vision-language model to classify neurological diseases without sharing patient brain MRI scans, using 7,641 scans from 67 sites across 12 countries covering Alzheimer's, stroke, and epilepsy—new sites only share noise-processed category summaries. Zero-shot classification AUC on 7 unseen sites reached 0.833-0.945, with training-free local prototypes lifting one site's epilepsy AUC from 0.508 to 0.762, approaching locally trained classifiers. This is a methodological preprint; accuracy gains from sharing are modest and not yet in clinical diagnosis.

### 4. Scientist-guided AI agent approaches strongest baseline in protein prediction

According to [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.21950), DiscoveryAgent is an AI agent framework letting scientists select, modify, or replace hypotheses between research rounds, paired with an audit agent checking each step's records. In protein variant effect prediction tasks, the scientist-guided version was surpassed by only one among 106 ProteinGym baselines; three domain case studies also identified hub genes in CD4+ T cells linked to autoimmune disease. This is a preprint; performance measured on specific benchmarks doesn't equal actual drug discovery success rates.

### 5. Single-cell perturbation prediction model generalizes better across contexts

According to [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.21959), OCCAM uses a multilayer perceptron framework to learn single-cell representations and predict gene perturbation effects—perturbations meaning experimental changes to a gene to observe cell responses. In "cross-context" few-shot prediction—where a perturbation and cell context were each seen but never together—OCCAM better captured unseen combination responses than four retrained opponents; for double perturbations changing two genes simultaneously, it combined single-gene responses to reduce expression prediction errors. This is a preprint method evaluation; accurately predicting expression levels remains challenging.

### 6. Dental CBCT imaging detects MRI-confirmed soft tissue lesions

According to [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.23042), researchers used physics-guided self-supervised learning to extract soft tissue lesion signals from routine dental CBCT (a cone-beam CT X-ray optimized for viewing bone) that typically require MRI to visualize. On 212 paired registered temporomandibular joint CBCT-MRI datasets, a special pretraining setup achieved joint effusion detection ROC AUC of 0.793, suggesting low-dose X-ray imaging may retain soft tissue information invisible to the human eye. This is a preprint retrospective study not yet prospectively validated and can't replace MRI diagnosis.

### 7. Genetic barcoding tracks human stem cell differentiation in bone marrow transplants

According to [media reports](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/translational-medicine/genetic-barcoding-reveals-how-pre-bone-marrow-transplant-conditioning-impacts-immune-system-regeneration/), a USC team used genetic barcodes to tag individual human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, transplanted them into mice, and tracked how they rebuilt blood and immune systems. The research found human multipotent progenitors have stronger long-term regenerative capacity than previously recognized in mouse experiments, while hematopoietic stem cells produced more blood across broader lineages; different pre-transplant conditioning (radiation vs. chemotherapy drug busulfan) affected immune cell output type and speed, with stem cells less affected. Results from human-mouse chimeric models; whether they directly guide patient transplant protocols requires further study.

### 8. Protein design energy function gets GPU-accelerated implementation

According to [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.21857), tmol is a GPU-native PyTorch library implementing the Rosetta protein energy function RosettaGenFF, with batch scoring throughput improved up to 362x, differentiable, and pluggable into machine learning frameworks. Using it as a reward signal for generative protein design improved designed interfaces in hydrogen bond and water bridge counts. This is a preprint; throughput and interface improvements measured on specific benchmarks, actual design success rates need more validation.

### 9. Bioengineered blood vessels enter first human test

According to [media reports](https://longevity.technology/news/humacytes-bioengineered-bypass-vessel-gets-first-human-test/), Humacyte launched a 10-person trial testing an "off-the-shelf" bioengineered vessel for use in heart bypass surgery, replacing autologous veins harvested from patients' legs. The vessel is manufactured by a North Carolina company, aiming to make bypass grafts possible without harvesting patients' own veins. This is a first-in-human trial, small scale with no safety or patency results published yet—far from widespread availability.

### 10. Curvature-weighted graph networks' gains in protein interaction prediction reinterpreted

According to [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.21849), researchers analyzed curvature-enhanced graph convolutional networks (CGCN) edge-by-edge on four protein interaction datasets, finding overall AUC, AUPR, and F1 improvements, but gains almost entirely from low-degree edges, while three datasets got worse on low-curvature (bottleneck) edges, contradicting the "alleviate over-squashing" motivation. Further analysis suggests curvature weighting actually sharpened inter-cluster separation, improving intra-cluster prediction at the cost of inter-cluster prediction. This is a preprint methodological analysis; conclusions limited to tested datasets.

### Does ImmunoBrain's Phase I data prove the drug works

No. According to [media reports](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/translational-medicine/beyond-amyloid-and-tau-alzheimers-drug-developer-sees-early-clinical-success-for-immunotherapy-based-approach/), Phase I primarily assesses safety, a 40-person sample has no statistical significance, biomarker drops are exploratory signals; the team has submitted Phase II to validate with clinical endpoints. Biomarker improvement doesn't equal cognitive function improvement or disease progression halted.

### What do partial reprogramming cell differences mean for existing trials

Can't directly extrapolate. That preprint tested OSKM four factors briefly activated in three human cell types in vitro, while Life Biosciences' optic nerve trial uses OSK three factors continuously for eight weeks—formula, duration, and target cells all differ, results can't be mutually inferred. According to [Lifespan.io reporting](https://lifespan.io/partial-reprogrammings-outcome-depends-on-cell-type/), the research reminds us to verify whether post-reprogramming cell function truly improves, not just whether epigenetic age drops.
