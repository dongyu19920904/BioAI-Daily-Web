## **🔥 Today's Top 9 Highlights**

### 1. Asprosin — a Protein Secreted by Fat Cells — Can Drive Vascular Aging

Asprosin, a protein produced by aging fat cells, damages mitochondria in vascular endothelial cells and triggers cellular senescence — the state where cells stop dividing and start pumping out inflammatory signals to their neighbors. That's according to [a report from Lifespan.io](https://lifespan.io/fat-cells-can-secrete-a-senescence-promoter/). In mouse experiments, injecting anti-Asprosin antibodies or knocking out the gene both led to measurable drops in arterial wall thickening and inflammatory markers. In vitro, conditioned media from fat cells engineered to skip Asprosin production caused less damage to human endothelial cells. This is still cell-and-animal-stage work — the researchers themselves note the full causal chain behind elevated Asprosin hasn't been worked out, and whether it beats existing vascular therapies is an open question for clinical trials. 🔬

### 2. AI Flags a Gut Microbiome Metabolic Signature Tied to Human Longevity

Researchers at UC San Diego identified specific shifts in gut microbiome metabolic activity in long-lived people, then used engineered bacteria to reproduce similar metabolic health improvements in aged mice — per [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261116/Gut-microbiome-changes-could-potentially-be-harnessed-to-support-healthier-aging.aspx). This is an association study; whether these metabolic patterns can be reliably amplified in humans, and whether that actually extends lifespan, still needs hard data from the original paper. 🦠

### 3. HEmIF-Bench — A Systematic Evaluation of AI Models That Translate H&E Slides to Multiplex Immunofluorescence

In pathology, multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) staining can label multiple proteins simultaneously and is great for analyzing tumor microenvironments, but routine H&E staining is cheaper and far more common. [The HEmIF-Bench paper](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.19905) benchmarks five generative AI models that "translate" H&E images into mIF across tasks including cell typing, tumor lymphoid structure detection, and patient survival prediction. The headline finding: pixel-level staining fidelity doesn't translate to downstream task performance, and none of the generative models consistently beat UNI2, a foundation model pretrained on large-scale H&E data. That's a reality check for tools sold on "accurate virtual staining" — real diagnostic value still needs independent validation. 🧫

### 4. MT-RGCN-DTI — A Drug-Target Interaction Prediction Model

MT-RGCN-DTI tackles one of pharma's early-stage bottlenecks: quickly predicting which drug molecules will bind to a target protein to cut experimental costs. [The paper](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.20109) uses a relational graph convolutional network — a graph neural network that handles multiple node and edge types — to jointly integrate drug similarity, protein similarity, and known interaction networks, hitting competitive predictive performance across several standard benchmarks. Code is open-sourced at [the GitHub repo](https://github.com/Dheeran-S/MT-RGCN-DTI/). It's a preprint reporting benchmark results, not a validated real-world drug pipeline. 💊

### 6. Agent Orange Exposure Raises Parkinson's Risk by 57% in Veterans

A study analyzing electronic health records from millions of Vietnam War veterans found that soldiers exposed to the defoliant Agent Orange had a 57% higher risk of Parkinson's disease and related conditions compared to those who weren't exposed — per [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261116/Agent-Orange-exposure-significantly-raises-Parkinsons-risk-in-veterans.aspx). This is large-scale observational research showing a statistical association, not a direct causal mechanism. The specific pathway between dioxins and other chemicals in Agent Orange and neurotoxicity wasn't covered in the abstract. ⚠️

### 7. Alzheimer's Research Finds Strong Links Between Physical and Brain Health

A study on Alzheimer's disease and aging found strong associations between physical health status and brain health — per [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261116/Research-on-Alzheimers-finds-strong-links-between-physical-and-brain-health.aspx). ### 8. SemiNovo — A Semi-Supervised Framework for De Novo Peptide Sequencing

In proteomics, recovering amino acid sequences directly from mass spectrometry data (de novo sequencing) is key to identifying unknown proteins, but most deep learning models train only on high-confidence spectra that databases can already match — leaving a huge amount of unannotated spectra on the table. [The SemiNovo paper](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.21079) builds a semi-supervised framework that learns from both annotated and unannotated spectra simultaneously, and also releases DarkSpec, a large-scale corpus of unannotated mass spectra. It achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks. Code and dataset are publicly available; the paper is a preprint. 🔭

### 9. AINN — Embedding Actuarial Constraints into a Longevity Prediction Neural Network

[The AINN paper](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.18802) bakes actuarial science's monotonicity prior — the assumption that mortality rates increase monotonically with age — into LSTM training as a differentiable penalty term, then applies it to lifespan prediction for six countries (Switzerland, Sweden, Norway, West Germany, the Netherlands, and Japan) using data from 1956–2020. The key finding is a quantifiable accuracy-stability tradeoff on the constraint strength coefficient λ: at λ=0.1, prediction confidence intervals narrow by 25% while RMSE increases by only 0.84%; 10-year out-of-sample backtesting yields a mean absolute error of 0.38 years. This is statistical modeling research — results depend on the selected countries and historical data, and say nothing about predicting any individual's lifespan. 📊

## **🧪 Frontier Research**

### A Parametric Analysis Framework for Corpus Callosum Diffusion MRI

The corpus callosum is the brain's largest white matter tract connecting the two hemispheres, and its microstructural changes are associated with aging and Alzheimer's disease. [This paper](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.19066) proposes a standardized grid resampling method for the midsagittal cross-section of the corpus callosum that establishes spatial correspondence across subjects without the computational cost of inter-subject registration. On aging and Alzheimer's datasets, the new method's effect size maps are broadly consistent with existing mainstream approaches while revealing localized differences those methods missed. It's a preprint reporting methodological validation — not yet a clinical diagnostic tool. 🧬

## **⌘ Top Open Source Projects**

### Shape-Lab/SPHARM-Mamba: A Cortical Surface Brain Age Prediction Tool

[SPHARM-Mamba](https://github.com/Shape-Lab/SPHARM-Mamba) is an open-source tool for cortical surface morphology analysis and brain age prediction. Its core approach combines spherical harmonics — a mathematical tool for describing shapes on a sphere — with the Mamba architecture, giving the model rotational invariance to brain orientation, making it suitable for multi-scale morphological analysis of brain regions using structural MRI data. Brain age is the brain's estimated "biological age" derived from neuroimaging, and it's widely used in aging and neurodegenerative disease research. The repo description covers its purpose; it has 1 star and was last active on November 9, 2026. License info is pending verification — check the repo for runtime requirements before using. ⭐
