## **🔥 Today's Top 8 Highlights**

### 1. One Shot, Two Years of Cholesterol Control — Primate Data Is In

Scribe Therapeutics made waves at the American Heart Association (AHA) annual meeting, announcing that a single injection of its experimental gene-editing therapy STX-1150 kept LDL cholesterol (the "bad" kind) at low levels for at least two years in non-human primates. Per [Longevity.Technology's coverage](https://longevity.technology/news/scribes-one-dose-approach-keeps-cholesterol-down-for-years/), the company also dropped early data on another candidate, STX-1200, at the same session. All results are still from animal studies — no human clinical trials yet. 🧬

### 2. Elamipretide Slows Ovarian Aging in Mice 🐭

Published in the peer-reviewed journal *Aging Cell*, [this study](https://lifespan.io/the-short-peptide-elamipretide-delays-ovarian-aging-in-mice/) found that seven consecutive days of injecting the short peptide Elamipretide into aging mice improved egg cell (oocyte) maturation quality, boosted fertilization rates, and increased litter sizes within one month post-treatment. Elamipretide also showed similar improvement signals in vitro on human oocytes from women over 35 — but the gap between in vitro experiments and real clinical outcomes is still massive. This doesn't mean the drug can extend the human fertility window just yet.

### 3. Yogurt and Cheese Don't Clearly Help Muscle Health in People 50+ 🥛

A cross-sectional analysis of 6,790 Irish adults aged 50 and over found that after controlling for lifestyle and socioeconomic factors, consuming fermented dairy products (yogurt and cheese) showed no consistent association with grip strength, physical performance, or frailty levels. Per [News-Medical's coverage](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261111/Yogurt-and-cheese-are-rich-in-protein-but-do-they-help-aging-muscles.aspx), one analysis even flagged a link between higher intake and elevated odds of low grip strength — though other analyses couldn't replicate that finding. Cross-sectional studies only capture associations at a single point in time, so causality is off the table. Researchers emphasized the need for longitudinal follow-up.

## **⌘ Top Open Source Projects**

### qlu-lab/SARDINE: A Statistical Framework for Studying Aging Phenotype Associations via Epigenetic Clocks

[SARDINE](https://github.com/qlu-lab/SARDINE) is an R framework for estimating associations between epigenetic clocks and various aging phenotypes from summary statistics in epigenome-wide association studies (EWAS). Epigenetic clocks are tools that estimate biological age from DNA methylation patterns. SARDINE's core value is that it works with publicly available summary statistics rather than requiring individual-level data — making it accessible to researchers without large raw cohorts. Last active in early November 2026, currently sitting at 1 star. Check the repo's docs and examples before diving in.

### anbai106/SleepChart: Modeling Scripts for Sleep Duration vs. 23 Biological Age Clocks

[SleepChart](https://github.com/anbai106/SleepChart) provides R scripts for analyzing the relationship between sleep duration and 23 biological age clocks, using Generalized Additive Models (GAM — a statistical method that captures non-linear relationships). It's a solid fit for data analysts working at the intersection of sleep and aging research. The repo includes the original analysis scripts, has 3 stars, and was last active in late October 2026. Check the repo page for license info before use.

## **❓ Related Questions**

### What's the Human Trial Status for Elamipretide — Can It Be Used Now?

Not yet. 🚫 In [this Aging Cell study](https://lifespan.io/the-short-peptide-elamipretide-delays-ovarian-aging-in-mice/), the human component was limited to in vitro experiments on cultured oocytes — not a human clinical trial. The in-vivo mouse results also can't be directly extrapolated to humans. Elamipretide has been studied in humans for heart failure before, but there's no registered clinical trial data available for the ovarian aging indication yet.
