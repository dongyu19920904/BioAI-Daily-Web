## **🔥 Top 8 Today**

### 1. 114 Rare Genetic Variants Found Behind Centenarians' Exceptional Memory

Researchers screened families of centenarians for "cognitive super-agers" — people over 100 whose memory matches that of a healthy adult in their 70s or 80s. [According to News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261123/Why-do-some-people-reach-100-with-memories-as-sharp-as-someone-30-years-younger.aspx), the study identified 114 rare genetic variants enriched in this group, touching genes related to vascular function, immune regulation, and neuronal structure. These variants hint at some potential genetic mechanisms, but the researchers were clear: direct comparisons within the same cohort didn't establish a significant association, so there's no confirmation yet that these variants protect against cognitive decline. Independent replication is still needed.

### 2. Liver Enzyme EPHX1 Drives Fibrosis — Animal Study Surfaces Blood Biomarker Clue

EPHX1 — an enzyme that metabolizes anti-inflammatory lipids into inactive byproducts — is the focus of new findings from a team at Osaka Metropolitan University. [According to GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/drug-discovery/metabolic-pathway-identified-as-potential-target-for-liver-diseases/), the enzyme was found at significantly elevated levels in both mouse models and livers of patients with advanced fibrosis. Mice with EPHX1 knocked out showed higher levels of the anti-inflammatory lipid 11,12-EET, along with reduced liver fibrosis and oxidative stress. Blood levels of 11,12-DHET (the metabolic product of EET) may serve as a biomarker for identifying patients with advanced fibrosis. No EPHX1 inhibitors for humans currently exist, and the stability and safety of candidate compounds still need to be validated — clinical application is a long way off.

### 3. RumiFold: AI Protein Folding Model That Combines Structure Prediction with Natural Language Q&A 🧬

RumiFold is a model trained on top of a general-purpose language model to handle protein structure folding. A preprint describes how it generates structural representations for each amino acid position through a multi-round folding process, then uses those representations to answer natural-language questions. It hits state-of-the-art on Gene Ontology protein function prediction, and its folding accuracy is on par with the specialized single-representation model SimpleFold. The [paper and code](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.29444) are both public, with the repo at rumifold.baulab.info. This is a preprint and hasn't been peer-reviewed — practical value awaits independent validation.

### 4. GABIF: Antibody Design Model That Uses Binding Affinity Predictions to Guide Sequence Generation 💉

GABIF is an antibody sequence design model built around a clever idea: use an antibody-antigen binding affinity predictor as a conditioning signal inside a discrete diffusion framework, nudging amino acid sequence generation toward higher affinity. A [preprint](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.29935) reports that sequences generated by the model showed improved binding affinity when verified by an independent predictor, with overall performance competitive with current best methods. The paper is upfront that all experiments are in silico — no wet-lab validation has been done, so this doesn't represent an actual improvement in drug efficacy yet.

### 5. LeCellModel: Single-Cell RNA Model That Finds Rare Cell States by Modeling Expression Distributions 🔬

LeCellModel shifts the goal of single-cell RNA sequencing representation learning from "identify cell types" to "model cell state distributions." Single-cell RNA sequencing is a technique that simultaneously measures gene activity across thousands of individual cells. A [preprint](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.30955) reports that the model outperforms existing methods on the scIB representation quality benchmark, reproduces a known pro-fibrotic macrophage program in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis without labels, and uncovers a CD177+ regulatory T cell subpopulation previously described only in tumor contexts. This is a preprint, and all findings come from computational analysis pending experimental validation.

### 6. Cross-Modal Generation: From E. coli Genome Sequence to Colony Microscopy Image 🦠

A [preprint](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.31132) describes a conditional generative model that converts full E. coli genome sequences directly into colony microscopy images. Training data came from 167,356 single-cell images across 22 clinical blood isolates under 4 antibiotic conditions. Adding synthetic images to real training sets improved antibiotic resistance classification accuracy, with particularly notable gains in recall for resistance classes. The model was trained on a small set of clinical isolates, and whether it generalizes to a broader range of strains and antibiotic combinations remains to be tested.

### 7. TB Drug Resistance Prediction: A Lightweight Model Built for Low-Resource Hospital Hardware 🏥

A [preprint](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.29143) presents an antibiotic resistance prediction model for Mycobacterium tuberculosis (the pathogen that causes tuberculosis). It uses 4-mer frequency features from whole-genome sequences — essentially counting how often each four-base combination appears in the genome — combined with a random forest classifier. On an independent test set, it achieves a mean AUC of 0.82 across 20 antibiotics. The model is explicitly designed to run on compute-constrained edge devices in hospitals, which makes it potentially useful in low- and middle-income countries. This is a preprint with no independent clinical validation yet.

### 8. Air Pollution May Damage Brain Immune Cells, with Ties to Dementia Risk 🧠

A research team at Case Western Reserve University found that fine particulate pollution from traffic and industry may alter the behavior of microglia — the brain's immune cells — with an observed association with dementia risk. [According to News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261123/Air-pollution-may-damage-brain-immunity-and-increase-dementia-risk.aspx), specific sample sizes, study design, and full data results weren't detailed in the coverage, so the complete methodology is worth checking. This is an associational finding — no causal conclusions can be drawn directly.

## **🧪 Frontier Research**

### Yoga Improves Balance and Strength, But Evidence for Anti-Aging Is Still Thin 🧘

Yoga's effects on older adults are the subject of a new narrative review — a systematic synthesis of existing research rather than a new experiment. [According to News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261123/Yoga-improves-strength-and-balance-but-can-it-actually-slow-aging.aspx), the review found encouraging signs across balance, flexibility, muscle strength, blood pressure, and cognitive-psychological health. That said, the researchers were explicit that the current evidence isn't strong enough to support claims that yoga slows biological aging or extends lifespan, and they're calling for larger, longer-term clinical trials.

### CAMEO: Fusing Pathology Images with Gene Expression When Paired Data Is Scarce 🔬

CAMEO is a framework for fusing two data modalities — pathology images and gene expression — when paired samples are extremely limited, which is a common bottleneck in biomedical multimodal learning. A [preprint](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.30601) reports that CAMEO uses an adversarial learning mechanism to align the latent representations of both modalities, outperforming contrastive methods like CLIP on cell niche classification and cell type composition prediction tasks. The team also released a HuggingFace dataset covering three organs with paired image and gene expression data. All results come from computational pathology benchmarks and await validation on broader biomedical tasks.

## **⌘ Top Open Source Projects**

### ai-2070/biomarker-tracker: Local Blood Marker and Biological Age Tracker

This tool helps individuals manage years of blood test results and health data entirely on their own machine. Core features include calculating PhenoAge — an algorithm that estimates biological age from blood markers, reflecting functional rather than chronological aging — generating plain-language explanations for each marker, and surfacing diet and lifestyle research with citations. It's designed to work offline with no account required, no data uploads, and no network calls. The [repo](https://github.com/ai-2070/biomarker-tracker) is public but has few stars, so real-world stability is still subject to community feedback. Worth noting: PhenoAge is a population-level statistical estimate, and a single result isn't a definitive prediction of individual disease risk.

### gangchen/epiage-skill: Offline Calculator for 24 Epigenetic Aging Clocks

[This repo](https://github.com/gangchen/epiage-skill) packages 24 epigenetic aging clocks — algorithms that estimate biological age from DNA methylation patterns, including GrimAge V1/V2, Horvath, Hannum, PhenoAge, DunedinPoAm, and more — as an installable offline agent skill. It only depends on pandas and numpy, making it well-suited for local research environments or air-gapped setups. Last active November 19, 2026, with 4 stars. License info and data input format requirements are worth checking in the repo README.

## **❓ Related Questions**

### My Estimated Biological Age Is Way Off From My Actual Age — Does That Mean Something's Wrong? 🤔

Not necessarily — that's not a valid direct interpretation. PhenoAge, GrimAge, and similar biological age scores are estimates from population-level statistical models. A single measurement is affected by timing, physical state, lab variability, and more. As the [gangchen/epiage-skill documentation](https://github.com/gangchen/epiage-skill) notes, the repo includes multiple different algorithms, each with its own prediction range and calibration population — the same person can get quite different results from different clocks. These tools are best used to track personal trends over time. They can't diagnose disease or replace a doctor's assessment.
