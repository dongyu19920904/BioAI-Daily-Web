### 1. Brain Serotonin Signaling Strengthens With Age, Suppressing Older Mice's Motivation to Move

A University of Aberdeen study published in *Metabolism* found that serotonin neurons in the dorsal raphe nucleus of aging mice fire more frequently during active periods. By activating inhibitory interneurons, this reduces dopamine release from the ventral tegmental area — dopamine being the key neurotransmitter that drives motivation to move. After researchers used a virus to selectively eliminate these serotonin neurons, mice aged 14–20 months showed improved activity levels, movement speed, and grip strength; body fat also dropped over eight weeks with no change in food intake. According to [a report by Lifespan.io](https://lifespan.io/serotonin-signals-suppress-physical-activity-in-aging-mice/), the researchers first confirmed declining activity patterns in Scottish volunteers aged 17–70 using accelerometers, validating the mouse model's relevance. These results are still limited to animal studies, and the viral neuron-elimination method isn't applicable to humans — future research needs gentler intervention strategies. 🐭🧠

### 2. AI-Estimated "Cardiac Age" Being Older Than Chronological Age Linked to Worse Health Outcomes

Research presented at the ESC Digital & AI Summit 2026 showed that AI analysis of echocardiograms can estimate a person's "cardiac age" — and when that estimate exceeds their actual age, it correlates with worse health outcomes. "Cardiac age" is a composite index of cardiac functional status, similar to the concept of "biological age," measuring whether the heart is aging faster than the calendar. According to [a report by News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261113/Older-AI-estimated-heart-age-linked-to-worse-health-outcomes.aspx), this remains an associational study; specific sample sizes and follow-up duration are pending verification, and it can't yet be said that the AI estimate itself can predict or change an individual's cardiac disease risk. ❤️

### 3. A Molecule Produced by Gut Probiotics Can Rapidly Repair Intestinal Wall Damage

Scientists at Rutgers School of Health discovered that a molecule produced by "good" gut bacteria can rapidly repair damaged intestinal mucosa and promote the production of a coenzyme that declines with age. The intestinal mucosa is the barrier that prevents bacteria and toxins from entering the bloodstream; damage to it is associated with serious conditions like inflammatory bowel disease. According to [a report by News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261113/Researchers-discover-bacterial-molecule-that-rapidly-repairs-intestinal-damage.aspx), the molecule's specific identity, experimental model (cell, animal, or human), and detailed data are still pending verification — but the research direction clearly sits at the intersection of gut microbiota, intestinal repair, and aging-related metabolism. 🦠

### 4. Merck Launches Phase 3 Trial Testing Whether COPD Maintenance Drug Can Reduce Acute Exacerbations

Ohtuvayre (ensifentrine), a COPD drug, received FDA approval in June 2024 for adult maintenance treatment — but the original approval trials didn't specifically measure whether it reduces "acute exacerbations," meaning sudden disease flare-ups. Merck has now launched a Phase 3 clinical trial called ENHANCE-3, planning to enroll approximately 2,136 patients in the US and other countries with one year of follow-up, directly addressing this question. According to [a report by Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/merck-tests-a-copd-drug-against-flare-ups/), the first patients have begun enrolling — though enrollment isn't the same as completing treatment, and results won't be available until the trial wraps up. 💊

### 5. NKGen Biotech Presents 12-Month Natural Killer Cell Therapy Data for a Single Alzheimer's Patient

NKGen Biotech presented a poster at the CTAD conference in Boston covering just one Alzheimer's patient, documenting 12 consecutive months of observation with natural killer cell therapy — a treatment approach that uses immune cells to target diseased cells. According to [a report by Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/one-alzheimers-patient-12-more-months-of-therapy/), a single-case report can't be compared against a control group and can't determine whether the therapy is effective; specific outcome data are pending verification. That said, this kind of early observation typically serves to provide safety and tolerability reference points for larger trials down the road. 🔬

## **◎ Industry Moves & What They Mean for You**

### Evommune Licenses Autoimmune Drug EVO570 for $30M Upfront, Phase 1 Trial Imminent

Evommune announced on November 4 that it licensed the oral drug EVO570 from Chengdu-based biotech Hinova Pharmaceuticals for $30 million upfront. EVO570 is a degrader targeting the VAV1 protein, intended for autoimmune disease treatment. VAV1 is a key signaling molecule in immune cell activation, and degrading it aims to suppress overactive immune responses. According to [a report by Longevity.Technology](https://longevity.technology/news/evommune-bets-30m-upfront-on-a-new-autoimmune-drug/), potential milestone payments could reach up to approximately $1.1 billion, and a Phase 1 human safety trial is about to kick off — though Phase 1 only evaluates safety and can't yet speak to clinical efficacy for patients. 💰

## **❓ Related Questions**

### If my "cardiac age" is older than my actual age, does that mean my heart has already aged ahead of schedule?

You can't jump to that conclusion directly. The AI-estimated "cardiac age" is a statistical index derived from echocardiographic image features, reflecting population-level associations. A single elevated reading correlates with worse health outcomes, but that doesn't mean any individual's heart has definitively experienced accelerated aging or disease. According to [a report by News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261113/Older-AI-estimated-heart-age-linked-to-worse-health-outcomes.aspx), what was released is an associational finding — the specific predictive power and clinical usage standards still need more research to nail down. ❤️‍🩹
