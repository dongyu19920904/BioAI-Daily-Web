## **🎯 Today's Top 10 Focus**

### 1. Protein Pocket Model Predicts Ligand Property Ranges

RangeFinder, per [Paper Index/Abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.03379), is an SE(3)-equivariant transformer that cuts straight to predicting the numerical ranges of nine ligand physicochemical properties from a protein binding pocket's structural, chemical, and evolutionary info — no per-molecule scoring needed. On the BioLiP2 dataset, it hits a range-IoU of 0.464 at just 12.2 milliseconds per pocket, beating all deployable baselines. Ablation experiments reveal that performance leans more on the density and quality of ligand supervision data than on any single input modality. The work is pitched as a low-cost molecular filtering prior before docking. Code is open-sourced at [GitHub Repo](https://github.com/alannadels/RangeFinder), but actual drug screening validation hasn't been done yet.

### 2. MRI Autism Classification Using a Typical Brain as Reference

NormMoX, per [Paper Index/Abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.03349), classifies autism from structural and functional MRI using normative modeling — basically, it first builds normal ranges for brain region metrics across age and sex in healthy folks, then checks how much an individual deviates. On 1,035 participants from ABIDE I, it lands an AUROC of 0.728, outperforming linear and deep fusion baselines. The model uses a "typical brain" as a shared reference, skips imputation when a modality is missing, and can point out exactly where each person deviates from the typical — by modality and brain region. The study is retrospective classification, though, with no clinical diagnostic validation. Code is open-sourced at [GitHub Repo](https://github.com/Novelex/NormMox).

### 3. Training-Free Method Finds High-Functioning Antimicrobial Peptides

Information-theoretic methods, per [Paper Index/Abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.03603), outperform variational autoencoders at finding rare high-functioning sequences — at least on two complete fitness landscapes. The kicker? No training required, and it runs significantly faster than standard ML/AI methods while being more effective at surfacing those rare gems. The team put the method to work hunting for antimicrobial peptides that specifically target bacterial cell membranes and leave eukaryotic membranes alone. Whether the antimicrobial activity and selectivity hold up in actual biological experiments still needs confirmation.

### 4. Single-Occurrence Facts in Training Data Cap a Model's Scientific Discovery Power

Monofacts — facts that show up exactly once during training — set the ceiling for a large language model's scientific discovery chops, per [Paper Index/Abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.03617). In materials science and regulatory genomics, the team directly tweaked the monofact rate in training sets and found that more monofacts lead to more discoveries across two open-source model families. You can't just explain this away with training set diversity, either. The relationship even transfers across scientific domains when paired with in-domain data. The catch: this was all done on controlled training sets, so whether it generalizes to general-purpose large models is still up in the air.

### 5. Jointly Recovering Cell Trajectories and RNA Dynamics

ManifoldVelo, per [Paper Index/Abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.03056), is a topology-constrained framework that jointly recovers pseudotime (an estimated time ordering of cellular processes based on gene expression) and RNA velocity (the direction of cellular change driven by transcription and degradation dynamics) on a continuous expression manifold. It nails the best pseudotime recovery and highest direction consistency in both multimodal periodic and branching simulations, and real-data analysis lines up with pancreatic developmental stages. New cells can be predicted using only spliced RNA — no need to retrain a reference model. Validated on simulated and single-cell data, but in vivo causal inference? Not yet.

### 6. Iterative Refinement Integrates Spatial Multi-Omics Data

ILR-SMO, per [Paper Index/Abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.02932), reframes spatial multi-omics integration (measuring multiple molecular types while keeping tissue spatial location intact) as a stability-aware refinement process over a shared latent representation — not a one-shot fusion. By progressively injecting each modality's information with reliability-aware gating and spatial consistency constraints, it beats seven existing methods across five spatial multi-omics benchmarks and stays more robust against modality imbalance and measurement noise. The framework is self-supervised, but validation is limited to public benchmark datasets. How it performs on actual clinical tissue samples remains to be seen.

### 7. Generating Visual Images Preferred by Artificial Networks and the Human Brain

Vis-Lens, per [Paper Index/Abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.03597), combines a variational autoencoder and a diffusion model into an image generation framework that directly produces natural images "preferred" by artificial neural network units or the human brain's visual cortex (using fMRI data) — no per-image iterative optimization needed. It pulls this off through activation-based prior regularization of latent codes. On both artificial neural networks and the human visual cortex, it outperforms recent methods in activation modulation capability and image realism. It's a representation visualization tool, though — no clinical diagnosis or therapeutic applications here.

### 10. Anti-Aging Vaccine Extends Mouse Median Lifespan by 12%

A three-element senolytic vaccine (yep, a vaccine that clears out senescent cells), per [Media Report](https://lifespan.io/rejuvenation-roundup-october-2026/), lowered aging markers and extended median lifespan by 12% when given to female B6 mice in late life. The vaccine goes after senescent cells, not pathogens. But heads up — results have only been seen in female mice, so there's no evidence it works in humans. The source is a Lifespan.io monthly roundup; original paper details are still pending verification.
