### 1. One CRISPR Injection, Three Years of Lower LDL — in Monkeys

Verve Therapeutics (now a Lilly subsidiary) published research in *Nature* showing that a single injection of a CRISPR base-editing therapy durably lowered LDL ("bad") cholesterol in monkeys. The therapy targets ANGPTL3, a liver-produced protein that blocks the breakdown of fat particles in the blood — switching it off lowers both LDL and triglycerides. In healthy monkeys at the high dose, non-HDL cholesterol dropped an average of 31.8% and LDL dropped 12.9% over three years, with no accumulated liver damage detected. Researchers also knocked out LDL receptors in four monkeys to simulate familial hypercholesterolemia; after 35 days, LDL fell from 458 to 247 mg/dL (roughly 46%), though it remained significantly elevated — meaning this pathway can't fully solve that genetic disease. Editing was confirmed not to pass to offspring across 738 pups. All data is from animals; whether this works for people with diet- or age-related high cholesterol still needs human trials to answer. See [Lifespan.io's coverage](https://lifespan.io/single-dose-gene-therapy-lowers-ldl-cholesterol-in-monkeys/) for the full experimental design and data.

### 2. Black Seed and Aging: A Review That Finds the Human Evidence Lacking

[News-Medical's report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261124/Black-seed-has-been-used-for-2000-years-Could-it-help-us-age-better.aspx) covers a narrative review that systematically surveyed the evidence on black seed (*Nigella sativa*) and its main active compound thymoquinone, covering oxidative stress, inflammation, metabolic dysfunction, neurodegeneration, and cellular senescence. Narrative reviews don't run new experiments — they aggregate existing literature to assess overall evidence quality. Black seed shows only "modest improvements" in cardiometabolic risk markers and cognitive measures in human studies, which isn't enough to support claims that supplementation extends healthy human lifespan. The herb has been in use for over 2,000 years, but that history doesn't substitute for modern clinical evidence. 🌿

### 3. Circadian Rhythms and Protein Quality Control: A New Link Found in Fruit Flies 🪰

A research team at KAIST (Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology) found in fruit flies that cells perform quality checks on proteins as they're being synthesized — filtering out defective ones — and that this quality control process participates in regulating the biological clock. The circadian clock governs sleep-wake cycles, and protein synthesis quality control wasn't previously on the list of known clock-regulating mechanisms. The specific findings and mechanistic details are pending independent verification; check [News-Medical's report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261125/KAIST-team-uncovers-link-between-protein-quality-control-and-sleep-wake-rhythm-regulation.aspx) for the underlying research. These results are from fruit flies, and whether they translate to mammals or humans remains unclear.

### Excess Body Fat Makes Perimenopause Symptoms Worse

[News-Medical's report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261125/New-study-links-excess-adiposity-to-heightened-somatic-symptoms-during-perimenopause.aspx) covers a new study on the link between excess adiposity (too much fat tissue — slightly different from simply being overweight) and the severity of somatic symptoms during perimenopause. Perimenopause is the transition period in the years before menopause, often accompanied by hot flashes, fatigue, and other physical symptoms. The relationship between BMI and menopausal symptoms has been studied before, but specific effects of fat distribution on perimenopausal symptoms are less well-characterized. Detailed sample size and quantitative results need verification from the full paper; as an observational study, it can establish association but not causation.

## **⌘ Top Open Source Projects**

### lucascamillomd/pyaging: A GPU-Accelerated Python Toolkit for Aging Clocks

[pyaging](https://github.com/lucascamillomd/pyaging) packages multiple GPU-optimized aging clock algorithms, letting researchers estimate biological age — how old cells actually are, as opposed to chronological age — from genomic or epigenetic data. It's a good fit for aging researchers with Python experience who need to process samples in bulk. The repo has 134 stars and was last active November 22, 2026. Supported data formats and runtime requirements are in the repo's README; biological age estimates are statistical measures and shouldn't be read as definitive predictions of individual disease risk. 🧬

### ZT135292/Dog\_blood\_transcriptomic\_aging\_clock: An Aging Clock Built on Dog Blood Transcriptomics 🐕

[This repo](https://github.com/ZT135292/Dog_blood_transcriptomic_aging_clock) provides complete code for building an aging clock from canine blood gene expression data, implementing and benchmarking six machine learning approaches for age prediction: elastic net, Lasso, random forest, XGBoost, support vector regression, and mixture of experts. Transcriptomic aging clocks infer biological age from which genes are active and how active they are; dog models are also commonly used as reference points in human aging research. The repo was last active November 19, 2026; star count was not provided and license status needs verification.
