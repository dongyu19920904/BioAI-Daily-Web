## **🔥 Today's Top 10 Highlights**

### 1. AI Agent Tracks Tumor Changes, Matching Radiologist Diagnoses 87% of the Time

LesionAgent is a training-free AI agent that reads CT scans from multiple patient timepoints, automatically identifies lesions, matches them across time, and judges tumor progression according to clinical guidelines. Across 4 public longitudinal CT datasets, it tracked more lesions and measured them more accurately than all automated baselines. In 23 clinical follow-ups, it agreed with radiologist response assessments 20 times, including every instance of response change. 🎯 The key improvement came from system architecture, not the model itself: using the same large language model but removing the designed processing pipeline dropped detection F1 from 78% to 41%. These are benchmarking-phase results — whether this can replace real radiology workflows still needs prospective clinical validation. Check out the paper details and napari viewer app at the [LesionAgent repository](https://github.com/MIC-DKFZ/LesionAgent), and find the full metrics in the [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.27876).

### 2. Ruby, an AI Drug Potency Prediction Model, Leads on Multiple Public Benchmarks

Ruby is a potency prediction model that relies on intermediate representations from the structural foundation model Pearl, skipping the need to compute binding poses for each individual compound. According to the [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.27015), Ruby ranks highest on early enrichment composite scores across 7 public hit-identification targets, outperforms comparison methods on average correlation across four benchmarks (OpenFE, CASP16, FEP+ 4, and OpenBind), and achieves zero-shot prediction leadership across 8 biochemical and cellular assays in the Genesis-Incyte collaboration. 💊 Training data includes proprietary data alongside the GenesisPotency-2026 dataset (CC BY-SA 4.0) released with the paper. All results are computational benchmark evaluations and have not yet translated into clinical candidates.

### 3. CrypticFlow, a New Protein Conformation Prediction Model, Nails Both Global Accuracy and Local Geometry

CrypticFlow uses a hierarchical flow-matching scheme: it first generates sparse backbone anchor points in Cartesian coordinate space, then recursively fills in details in internal coordinate space. According to the [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.27162), it achieves the lowest median and mean Cα RMSD among same-parameter baselines on the D3PM test set, with adjacent distance fidelity hitting 99.58%. 🔬 Predicting the protein-drug bound conformation (the "holo" state) is notoriously harder than predicting the unbound state, and existing models either sacrifice local atomic geometry for global accuracy or accumulate errors over chain length — CrypticFlow targets both. Whether predicted conformations actually improve hit rates in real drug design remains unvalidated.

### 4. A New Aging Theory Unifies DNA, RNA, Proteins, and Metabolites in One Framework

A paper published in *The Innovation Life* proposes a "Central Dogma of Aging" that adds metabolites as a fourth layer to the classic information flow of DNA → RNA → Protein. The framework argues that aging results from the gradual breakdown of balance between synthesis, modification, and degradation across all four molecular layers. 🧬 Notably, RNA is cast not just as a passive messenger — defects in transcription, splicing, modification, and localization are argued to independently drive downstream protein homeostasis disruption. Metabolic changes like NAD⁺ decline and pro-inflammatory lipid accumulation are framed as drivers, not just downstream effects. The authors acknowledge that the field has no consensus on the relative importance of each mechanism, and the framework still needs experimental testing. Full paper at [doi:10.59717/j.xinn-life.2026.100262](https://doi.org/10.59717/j.xinn-life.2026.100262).

### 5. Tissue Structure Features from Pathology Slides Add Predictive Power on Top of AI Foundation Models

Researchers built a 139-dimensional feature set covering cell nucleus morphology, intercellular connections, and multi-cell community organization, then fused these with representations from two pathology foundation models, CTransPath and PLIP. According to the [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.27881), estrogen receptor classification on the BCNB breast cancer dataset reached a ROC-AUC of 0.9144 — higher than using foundation models alone — with similar gains on lung tissue typing. 🔍 The question being answered here is whether hand-crafted tissue structure metrics still add value on top of what foundation models have already learned, and the answer appears to be yes, at least on these datasets. Whether these interpretable features hold up across broader cancer types and institutions needs further study.

### 6. R2RG Improves 3D Brain MRI Representation Learning on Small Datasets

R2RG combines convolutional neural networks with a generative adversarial network (GAN) for reconstruction-based representation learning. According to the [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.27057), it outperforms contrastive representation learning methods on Parkinson's and Alzheimer's disease classification across three standard datasets and one locally collected dataset. 🧠 The core challenge in 3D MRI representation learning is that data is scarce, dimensionality is high, and slicing volumes into 2D loses spatial relationships — mainstream Transformer and diffusion architectures tend to struggle in this small-data regime. This is a benchmarking result and doesn't yet speak to clinical diagnostic accuracy improvements.

### 7. Albumin Modification Patterns Reveal a "Middle-Age Turning Point" in Biological Aging

Albumin, the most abundant protein in blood, undergoes post-translational modifications (chemical changes that happen after protein synthesis), and the patterns of these modifications may reflect the body's biological aging state. According to [News-Medical's report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261120/Albumin-modifications-reveal-new-clues-about-biological-aging.aspx), a paper published November 4, 2026 in *Aging* (Volume 18) maps how albumin modifications shift with age and identifies a critical turning point window in middle age. ⏳ The source provides only abstract-level information — specific sample sizes, the age range of the turning point, and modification types need to be confirmed in the original paper.

### 8. Mouse Study Maps the Genetic Landscape of Immune Aging

A research team at Monash University used preclinical mouse models to map the genetic basis by which the immune system loses its ability to respond to threats as it ages, according to [News-Medical's report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261123/Preclinical-mouse-models-reveal-how-aging-impairs-immune-threat-adaptation.aspx). 🐭 The immune system's declining ability to recognize and respond to cancer and infection with age is well-known, but the gene-level mechanisms behind it aren't. These results come from mouse experiments and need follow-up research to determine whether they translate to humans.

### 9. Corsera Health Launches Phase 1 Cardiovascular RNAi Prevention Drug Trial in Australia and New Zealand

Corsera Health has kicked off a Phase 1 clinical trial in Australia and New Zealand evaluating COR-2003 alone and in combination with COR-1004 as a cardiovascular disease prevention therapy. RNAi (RNA interference) works by disrupting specific gene expression to reduce the production of harmful proteins — it's still early-stage territory in cardiovascular prevention. 💉 Phase 1 trials are primarily about safety and tolerability, not efficacy. Full details at [Longevity.Technology's report](https://longevity.technology/news/corsera-health-begins-phase-1-trial-of-dual-rnai-preventive-medicine/).

### 10. New Adaptive Optics Imaging Tech Detects Glaucoma Progression Months Earlier

FDA researchers developed a high-resolution adaptive optics imaging technology that showed potential to detect signs of glaucoma progression months ahead of existing methods in patients at the University of Maryland, according to [News-Medical's report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261120/New-imaging-technology-detects-glaucoma-progression-months-earlier.aspx). 👁️ Glaucoma's optic nerve damage often advances silently before patients notice any vision changes, making early detection critical. The source is abstract-level — specific sample sizes, the time advantage over current methods, and false positive rates need to be confirmed in the original paper. This is promising early-stage research.

### COVID-19 Vaccines Carry a Higher Shoulder Injury Risk Than Flu Vaccines, Though the Absolute Difference Is Small

SIRVA (Shoulder Injury Related to Vaccine Administration) occurs when a vaccine is injected too high or at the wrong angle, causing the fluid to enter the shoulder joint and trigger pain and restricted movement. A study covering nearly two million doses found that COVID-19 vaccines carry a 63% higher relative risk of SIRVA compared to flu vaccines, though the absolute risk increase is small, according to [News-Medical's report](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261123/COVID-19-vaccines-carry-a-higher-shoulder-injury-risk-than-flu.aspx). 💉 The source is abstract-level — specific incidence rates by vaccine type need to be confirmed in the original paper.

### Coding Agent Hits Clinical Pathway Benchmarks with TEA-Bench-BR

TEA-Bench-BR is a coding benchmark built around Brazil's early autism screening clinical pathway, containing 144 tasks and 6,798 hidden test cases with FHIR R4 medical data standard export support. According to the [paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2611.27895), Gemma 4 31B achieves a 77.8%–85.2% solve rate on single-tool tasks, with errors concentrated in input validation rather than clinical logic. The 12B model misses 10.1% of referral-required cases; the 31B model misses just 0.02%. 🏥 The core problem this addresses is "silent errors" in clinical rule-to-code translation — programs that run fine and look reasonable but make wrong judgments. This benchmark helps quantify model reliability on rule translation before deployment, though it uses simulated instruments rather than real clinical tools.
