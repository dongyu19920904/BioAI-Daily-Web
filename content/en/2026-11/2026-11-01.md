## **🔥 Top 2 Today's Highlights**

### 1. A Blood Test May Warn of Alzheimer's Risk Up to 10 Years Before Symptoms

A large-scale international study suggests that a simple blood test could flag high-risk individuals years before cognitive decline kicks in — according to [this ScienceDaily report](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261029072142.htm). The riskiest group had roughly a 38% chance of developing cognitive impairment within 5 years, jumping to ~78% within 10, hinting that early intervention might actually be on the table. 🧪 That said, this is a media summary — it doesn't name the specific biomarkers being tested or report the sample size, so the original paper needs a closer look before drawing firm conclusions.

### 2. Aging Reshapes the Distribution and Stability of Memory Engram Cells

Memory engram cells — the specific neurons in the brain that store individual memories — are directly affected by aging, according to [a paper published in *Nature Communications*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-79049-9). 🧠 The research team found that aging alters the spatial distribution, stability, and gene expression profiles of these cells, suggesting the physical foundation of memory storage itself changes with age — not just the retrieval process. The paper is peer-reviewed, but since we only have the title right now, the experimental subjects, sample size, and quantitative findings still need to be confirmed from the full text.
