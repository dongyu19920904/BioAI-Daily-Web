### 1. Hypothalamic Neuron Cilia Length Affects Whole-Body Metabolism and Caloric Restriction Efficacy

According to [a report by Lifespan.io](https://lifespan.io/how-some-neuronal-cilia-may-affect-the-whole-body/), researchers found that cilia on hypothalamic neurons — those antenna-like structures cells extend from their surface — shorten with age, with declining NAD+ levels as the direct culprit. In vitro experiments showed that supplementing NAD+ precursors or inhibiting CD38, the enzyme that depletes it, could restore cilia length. The CHMP4B protein inside cilia normally cycles back to lysosomes to keep them intact, but that process breaks down when NAD+ runs low. More critically, when researchers knocked out cilia growth genes using silencing RNA, caloric restriction in 24-month-old mice stopped improving certain metabolic markers — pointing to these specific neuronal cilia as a necessary piece of the caloric restriction puzzle. The work was published in a peer-reviewed journal, but it's all in mice so far, and whether human hypothalamic cilia play the same role is still an open question.

### 2. Nvidia and UW Open-Source RFD4-Proteina Protein Design Model 🧬

RFD4-Proteina, an open-source generative model for protein design, was just unveiled by the UW Institute for Protein Design — led by Nobel laureate David Baker — in collaboration with Nvidia, [according to GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/nvidia-uw-institute-for-protein-design-unveil-open-source-protein-design-ai-model/). Built on Nvidia's BioNeMo platform, the model can simultaneously optimize protein sequences, structures, and their interactions with other molecules. Lab tests across four task categories — protein binding, small molecule binding, enzyme scaffolding, and DNA binding — showed RFD4-Proteina hitting 5–10x higher design success rates than baseline methods, and generating results 5–10x faster. The model is open-sourced under a BSD-3 license and accessible via Amazon Bio Discovery, Hugging Face, and Google Cloud. Baker says it'll help design proteins for drug discovery and enzymes that break down pesticides, though the road from computational design to lab validation is still a long one.

### 3. Cryo-EM and AI-Designed Scaffold Reveal How Andes Hantavirus Hijacks Human Cells 🦠

Researchers used cryo-EM to resolve the near-atomic-resolution structure of how the Andes virus spike protein binds to the human receptor protocadherin-1 (PCDH1), [according to GEN](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/infectious-diseases/cryo-em-and-ai-designed-scaffold-reveal-how-andes-hantavirus-binds-cells/). Andes virus is the only hantavirus known to spread person-to-person, and it can cause a deadly pulmonary syndrome. The team found that four Gn glycoprotein subunits on the virus surface form a deep pocket, into which PCDH1 inserts itself in a slender configuration — a geometry that most antibodies are simply too bulky to access. An AI-designed scaffold was used to stabilize this tetrameric complex. Mutagenesis experiments confirmed three Gn loops are involved in binding, and researchers even managed to transfer this binding capability to an Old World hantavirus. Published in *Nature*, the authors suggest designing slim molecules to block that deep pocket as a potential antiviral strategy — though no drug candidates exist yet.

## **🧪 Cutting-Edge Research**

### Trion-1: A Diffusion Language Model Unifying Language, Protein Sequences, and Structure

This [arXiv preprint](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.31215) introduces Trion-1, the first natively unified multimodal protein diffusion language model (dLLM). Trion-1 represents sequences, structures, and natural language in a single unified discrete token space, swapping out strict left-to-right generation for bidirectional or semi-bidirectional interaction — allowing global residues to communicate and model long-range contacts. This design unifies six core capabilities in one shot: folding, inverse folding, sequence understanding, structure understanding, sequence design, and structure design, while keeping the instruction-following ability of a general-purpose LLM. On benchmarks, Trion-1 hits a TM-score above 0.8 with a single sample (where TM-score measures predicted-vs-actual structural similarity, with >0.5 indicating the same fold) — comparable to existing three-modality models using 200 samples. On sequence understanding tasks it reaches 62.37%, putting it close to Claude Opus 4.7. It's a preprint, so no peer review yet; code and model weights are available via the paper link.

### Visual Grounding Benchmark Evaluates Multimodal LLMs on Retinal OCT 👁️

This [arXiv preprint](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.31205) introduces RetinaGroundBench, the first benchmark for visual grounding of biomarkers in retinal optical coherence tomography (OCT). OCT is the go-to imaging technique in ophthalmology, capable of showing the layered structure of the retina. The benchmark includes 815 B-scan images annotated by ophthalmologists with 4,537 bounding boxes covering 13 biomarkers across 8 diseases. The team evaluated 27 open-source multimodal LLMs (0.8B–38B parameters) and found visual grounding is nowhere near solved: the best general model hit a macro F1 of just 23.7%, with consistently low recall — even though accuracy on biomarker presence/absence questions reached 73%. The authors' own RetGrounder (8B parameters), fine-tuned with OCT-specific strategies, pushed F1 up to 33.9%, a 43% relative improvement. Still a preprint, and not yet validated in a clinical setting; code and model are available via the paper.

[CosinorAge](https://github.com/ADAMMA-CDHI-ETH-Zurich/CosinorAge) is a Python package implementing cosinor-based biological age prediction. Circadian rhythms — the body's ~24-hour physiological cycles — shift in amplitude and phase as we age, making them a viable aging biomarker. This tool is a solid fit for researchers who need to estimate biological age from time-series physiological data like body temperature or activity levels.
