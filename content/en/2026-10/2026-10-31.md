## **🔥 Today's Top 10 Highlights**

### 1. Anti-Aging Vaccine Extends Elderly Mouse Lifespan by 12%

A [mouse study](https://lifespan.io/an-anti-senescence-vaccine-increases-mouse-lifespan/) published in *Advanced Science* injected a three-component nanoparticle vaccine called CK5 into 75-week-old (already elderly) female C57BL/6 mice. The goal: train the immune system to recognize and clear "zombie cells" — senescent cells that have stopped dividing but keep pumping out pro-inflammatory signals that damage surrounding tissue. After vaccination, senescence markers in visceral fat dropped, blood sugar regulation improved, and median lifespan came in 12% longer than untreated controls. Two big caveats, though: each group had only 10 mice, and the control group's lifespan was shorter than typical for this strain, which may have inflated the difference. Larger independent studies are needed, and this says nothing about effectiveness in humans yet. 🧫

### 2. NEXT-Cue AI Tool Helps Assess Five-Year Alzheimer's Progression Risk in Mild Cognitive Impairment

NeuroXT, a South Korean company, announced that its AI software **NEXT-Cue** has received US FDA [510(k) clearance](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/neuroxt-inc-receives-us-fda-510k-clearance-for-next-cue-ai-software-supporting-prognostic-assessment-in-mild-cognitive-impairment-302913386.html) — a pre-market review pathway, not a full efficacy approval. The tool combines brain MRI imaging, demographic data, and cognitive test scores to generate supporting assessment information for clinicians evaluating patients with mild cognitive impairment (the stage where thinking shows subtle decline but daily life is still manageable), helping them gauge the risk of progressing to Alzheimer's dementia within five years. The announcement doesn't include accuracy, sample size, or other performance metrics. The company says it will build real-world evidence during commercialization, and according to [Longevity.Technology's coverage](https://longevity.technology/news/fda-clears-neuroxts-ai-tool-that-sizes-up-alzheimers-risk/), a Korean market authorization is also in the works. 🧠

### 3. Prenuvo's 14,031-Person Full-Body MRI Study Links Sleep Efficiency to Deep Belly Fat

**Prenuvo**, a full-body MRI screening company, teamed up with the University of Texas at Dallas to analyze data from 14,031 adults (average age 50). The findings: higher sleep efficiency — the ratio of actual sleep time to time spent in bed — correlates with less visceral fat (the deep fat wrapping your organs, more closely tied to metabolic risk) and greater parietal gray matter volume. On the flip side, people sleeping more than 8 hours per night had smaller hippocampal volume (the brain region associated with memory). These are correlational findings — you can't pin down causation: sleeping more might be a *consequence* of some underlying health issue, not the cause. Also worth noting: participants were paying screening customers, and sleep data came from self-reported questionnaires, not a sleep lab. Results don't generalize directly. Full details in [Longevity.Technology's coverage](https://longevity.technology/news/mri-study-of-14031-adults-ties-sleep-to-belly-fat/). 😴

### 4. Hypothalamic Neuronal Cilia: A Key Middleman in How Caloric Restriction Slows Aging

A mouse study published in *Advanced Science*, [covered by Lifespan.io](https://lifespan.io/how-some-neuronal-cilia-may-affect-the-whole-body/), found that primary cilia — tiny antenna-like structures projecting from neurons in the hypothalamus (the brain's metabolic control center) — shorten with age, partly because NAD+ levels (a coenzyme involved in cellular energy metabolism) decline. Caloric restriction can maintain cilia length by boosting NAD+, which in turn improved metabolic markers in elderly mice. When the researchers used gene silencing to block cilia growth in the hypothalamus of old mice, the metabolic benefits of caloric restriction disappeared. Results are currently limited to cell and mouse experiments — whether cilia could serve as a metabolic intervention target in humans needs more research. 🔬

### 5. Gene Editing Progress: AI Optimizes Prime Editing and Expands Its Disease Range

**Dr. David Liu** presented at the European Society of Gene and Cell Therapy (ESGCT) 2026 Annual Congress, sharing that his team used an AI tool called ProteinMPNN to redesign key proteins in prime editing (a technique that can precisely rewrite individual genetic "letters"), producing a variant called PE8 that solved the stability issues plaguing earlier evolved editors. Another machine learning tool, OptiPrime (available to try at the [opti.prime website](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/genome-editing/no-nonsense-david-liu-lauds-progress-in-disease-agnostic-gene-editing-at-esgct/)), helps researchers identify the best guide RNAs for editing. On the clinical side, the first patient received an ex vivo prime editing treatment in 2025, and the first in vivo prime editing dose was administered this October. Liu also introduced a method called PERT, which reprograms tRNA molecules (responsible for translating genetic information) to bypass a whole class of mutations shared across multiple genetic diseases — restoring about 7% of normal enzyme activity in a mouse model of Hurler syndrome. Still early proof-of-concept stage. 🧬

### 6. Blocking ANO6 Protein Slows Pancreatic Tumor Growth and Reduces Immune Suppression in Mice

**Fox Chase Cancer Center** researchers published a [mouse study](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/cancer/blocking-protein-slows-pancreatic-tumor-growth-and-reduces-immune-suppression-in-mice/) in *Nature Cancer* showing that cancer-associated fibroblasts (cells forming the tumor's "support tissue") use a protein called ANO6 to feed cholesterol and other lipids to cancer cells, while also causing tumor-infiltrating killer T cells to accumulate excess cholesterol and "exhaust" into ineffectiveness. In mouse models, inhibiting ANO6 activity with existing drugs significantly reduced tumor weight and improved T cell activity. The researchers note these drugs may also work through mechanisms independent of ANO6, and lipid transfer isn't the only nutrient pathway for tumors. Still animal-only at this stage. 🐭

### 7. Type 2 Diabetes: Intensive Diet + Exercise Put 54% of Under-45 Patients Into Remission Within Six Months

According to [Science Daily's coverage](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261029072129.htm), a study found that type 2 diabetes patients under age 45 who underwent an intensive intervention combining low-calorie diet with structured exercise achieved remission at a rate of 54% within six months — meaning blood sugar returned to levels where no medication was needed. The control group receiving standard care hit just 4%. The original abstract only provides this headline number; specific sample size, follow-up duration, intervention details, and peer-review status are pending verification. Readers should check the original source for confirmation. 💪

### 8. "Blue Zone" Longevity Village Certification Needs Independent Verification and Expiration Dates

**Eva Novak**, founder of Decades, published a commentary in Longevity.Technology reviewing the controversy surrounding "Blue Zones" — regions with unusually high concentrations of centenarians. Demographer Saul Newman previously noted in a preprint that extreme old-age records closely correlate with missing birth records. A village in Sardinia called Bolotana was recently nominated as a new Blue Zone, with a 13.8% probability of women surviving from age 70 to 100 between 2021–2025 — but this estimate comes from a village of only ~2,300 people, with a confidence interval spanning 7.3%–23.8%, and several co-authors have commercial ties to Blue Zones, Inc. The piece calls for independent demographers in the certification process, public case-by-case verification data, and periodic expiration-date-style re-reviews of Blue Zone designations. The [original article](https://longevity.technology/news/every-blue-zone-should-come-with-an-expiry-date/) links to primary papers and the AFAR redefinition document for deeper evidence-checking. 🌍

### 9. Dog Aging Project Lands $29M NIH Renewal Grant

**The Dog Aging Project** has secured approximately $29 million over five years in renewal funding from the National Institutes of Health, led by Tufts University, with nearly 55,000 companion dogs enrolled since 2018, according to [Longevity.Technology's coverage](https://longevity.technology/news/old-dogs-new-funding-dog-aging-project-fetches-29m-from-nih/). The project's core logic: dogs share our living environments, breathe the same air, drink the same water, develop age-related diseases like cognitive decline — and they go through an entire lifespan at a much faster pace, making them an effective model for studying how environmental exposures interact with aging. The next phase adds drinking water analysis, air pollution patches, activity trackers, and genetic sample collection from "super-longevity" dogs, aiming to move from "documenting what" to "explaining why." Dog findings can't be directly extrapolated to human interventions — longitudinal follow-up is still needed to establish causation. 🐕

### 10. Delta Wave Intrusions During REM Sleep Linked to Tau, Amyloid, and Memory Forgetting

A [study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78425-9) published in *Nature Communications* reports that delta wave intrusions during REM sleep (the dreaming stage) in older adults — slow brain waves that normally appear only in deep sleep — are associated with tau protein and amyloid accumulation (two hallmark pathological features of Alzheimer's disease) as well as degree of forgetting. This is abstract-level information; specific sample size, study design, and effect sizes need verification from the full paper. Currently correlational only — no causal conclusions or clinical recommendations can be drawn from this. 🧠

## **⌘ Top Open Source Projects**

### ADAMMA-CDHI-ETH-Zurich/CosinorAge: Predicting Biological Age with Circadian Rhythms

[CosinorAge](https://github.com/ADAMMA-CDHI-ETH-Zurich/CosinorAge) is a Python package from ETH Zurich built on the "cosinor" method — estimating biological age by analyzing a person's circadian rhythm patterns. This is a different angle from the more common methylation- or blood-marker-based clocks. It's a solid fit for researchers and developers with Python chops who want to do biological age research on wearable or diary data. The repo has 13 stars and was last active on October 25, 2026. Check the repo README for specific data format requirements and validation status. ⭐

### gangchen/epiage-skill: 24 Epigenetic Aging Clocks Packaged as an Offline-Ready Agent Tool

[epiage-skill](https://github.com/gangchen/epiage-skill) wraps 24 mainstream epigenetic aging clocks (including GrimAge V1/V2, Horvath, Hannum, PhenoAge, and more) into an installable, offline-capable agent skill module — just pandas and numpy, no network calls needed. Epigenetic clocks estimate biological age by analyzing DNA methylation patterns (chemical modifications that change with age without altering the underlying genetic sequence). Great for data scientists who want to run multiple clocks locally in bulk for comparative research. The repo has 3 stars, last active October 24, 2026. Check the repo documentation for runtime environment details and license info. 🔬

## **❓ Related Questions**

### Can mouse study results from the anti-aging vaccine be directly applied to humans?

Nope. [This study](https://lifespan.io/an-anti-senescence-vaccine-increases-mouse-lifespan/) observed a 12% lifespan extension in groups of just 10 mice, with a shorter-than-normal control group lifespan that may have inflated the effect. Mouse aging biology differs from humans in important ways, and many interventions that worked in mice have failed to replicate in human trials. This research is at the animal proof-of-concept stage — there's no human safety or efficacy data yet. Readers can dig into the methodology discussion around the short-lived control group issue in the references section of the original Lifespan.io article. 🐭
