## **🔥 Top 10 Stories**

### 1. [Talus Bio Releases First Structure-Free AI Drug Target Prediction Model](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/artificial-intelligence/talus-bios-structure-free-ai-model-targets-unstructured-proteins-in-their-native-cellular-context/)

About 40% of the human proteome lacks stable 3D structure, can't be resolved by AlphaFold, and can't be targeted using traditional drug development methods. Ptarmigan-1, released this week by Talus Bio, is the first AI model that skips protein structure prediction entirely and directly predicts small-molecule binding sites. Trained on five years of proprietary company data, it can screen over 3 billion compounds against the entire human proteome in one day—5000× faster than structure-based methods. In STAT6 inhibitor validation, the model outperformed structure-based approaches, with binding candidates confirmed by a third-party lab. The company currently offers limited free access; the core technology is the MARMOT platform—label-free measurement of proteins in their native cellular environment.

**Source Type**: Industry news / **Evidence Stage**: Model validation + in vitro experiments / **Credibility**: Medium (preprint, not peer-reviewed; in vitro validation by third-party lab, but no human trial data yet)

### 2. [Harvard Medical School Hosts Fourth Biomarkers of Aging Conference](https://lifespan.io/global-leaders-in-geroscience-convene-for-biomarkers-of-aging/)

On October 5-6, the Biomarkers of Aging Consortium will hold its annual meeting at Harvard Medical School. This year's agenda covers epigenetic clocks (Steve Horvath), pace-of-aging measurement (Dan Belsky), AI and multi-omics analysis, immunity and cellular senescence, and regulatory pathways (FDA, ARPA-H), among other critical topics. The consortium spans over 50 institutions and is dedicated to establishing standardized, clinically validated aging measurement methods. Satellite workshops on brain aging and reproductive aging will run concurrently. The consortium also operates the open-source platform Biolearn and the longitudinal study The Longevity Study.

**Source Type**: Conference announcement / **Evidence Stage**: Industry consensus-building platform / **Credibility**: High (authoritative academic and regulatory bodies involved, but does not directly release clinical trial results)

### 3. [Treadmill Training Delays Ovarian Aging in Mice via Adiponectin](https://lifespan.io/physical-activity-delays-ovarian-aging-in-mice/)

After one month of running, adult female mice had significantly higher numbers of primordial follicles and elevated anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH, a marker of ovarian reserve) levels compared to controls. The research team found that exercise increased adiponectin levels, which slows follicle activation by inhibiting the mTOR signaling pathway. The protective effect of exercise was markedly diminished in adiponectin-deficient mice. Next, researchers treated middle-aged mice with the adiponectin receptor agonist AdipoRon—not only was ovarian reserve protected, but reproductive lifespan was significantly extended: when the control group had nearly ceased reproduction, the treatment group was still giving birth. This provides a drug target for non-exercise intervention in ovarian aging.

**Source Type**: Research report / **Evidence Stage**: Animal experiments (mice) / **Credibility**: Medium (clear mechanism with drug validation, but not verified in humans; adiponectin's role in human ovarian aging remains unclear)
