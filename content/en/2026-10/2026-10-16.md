## **📎 Today's Citable Takeaways**

**Fact #1**: The MIT Picower Institute team published a peer-reviewed paper in *Cell Stem Cell*, using a genome-wide CRISPR knockout screen to identify suppressed Wnt signaling as the core mechanism behind APOE4-driven lipid droplet accumulation in oligodendrocytes and reduced myelination. In APOE4 Alzheimer's mouse models, treatment with GSK3b inhibitor CHIR99021 reduced hippocampal lipid droplets and improved myelination.
**Original source**: [How Alzheimer's Risk Gene APOE4 Disrupts Myelination](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/translational-medicine/how-alzheimers-risk-gene-apoe4-disrupts-myelination/)
**Evidence boundaries**: Peer-reviewed paper; experimental systems are human iPSC-derived cell cultures + transgenic mouse models; no human clinical trial data — cannot infer that GSK3b inhibitors are effective in human patients.

**Fact #2**: A *Lancet Neurology* cohort study tracked 130 blood proteins in patients receiving lecanemab, finding that amyloid-clearance-related proteins and cognition-preservation-related proteins followed strikingly different trajectories — suggesting the two are driven by distinct biological processes.
**Original source**: [Blood clues to why Alzheimer's drug results vary](https://longevity.technology/news/blood-clues-to-why-alzheimers-drug-results-vary/)
**Evidence boundaries**: Observational longitudinal cohort study; samples from a single center (Washington University Memory Diagnostic Center), non-randomized; detection platform provided by Alamar Biosciences, a company with a financial stake in the research; cannot yet predict who will benefit from treatment.

**Fact #3**: The MolHop preprint proposes organizing ~1.6M commercial molecular scaffolds into a hierarchical action space, letting an AI agent navigate between scaffolds via reinforcement learning — jumping across chemical space rather than optimizing around known structures. MolHop-9B outperforms baselines including GPT-5.6-sol, Claude-5-Opus, and Gemini-3.1-Pro on cross-scaffold joint success rate.
**Original source**: [MolHop: Scaffold-Hopping Molecular Design with Agentic Reinforcement Learning](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.19920)
**Evidence boundaries**: arXiv preprint, not yet peer-reviewed; benchmarked on standard datasets, not validated in a real drug discovery pipeline.

## **🔥 Top 10**

**1. [How APOE4 Wrecks the Brain's "Insulation" — MIT's CRISPR Screen Finds the Key Pathway](https://www.genengnews.com/topics/translational-medicine/how-alzheimers-risk-gene-apoe4-disrupts-myelination/)**

Picture the insulating sheath around a neuron's wiring starting to peel off — that's literally what's happening in the brains of people carrying the APOE4 gene. 🧠 The MIT Picower Institute's Li-Huei Tsai lab ran a genome-wide CRISPR screen, knocking out every single protein-coding gene in human cells one by one to systematically hunt down the culprit: Wnt signaling gets suppressed, causing oligodendrocytes — the cells responsible for "insulating" neurons — to pile up fat as lipid droplets instead of laying down myelin. The team validated this in both human cell cultures and Alzheimer's mouse models: restore Wnt activity with a GSK3b inhibitor, and lipid droplets drop while myelination recovers. This is the most systematic mechanistic dissection of APOE4-driven myelination damage to date, with GSK3b now on the map as a potential drug target — though human clinical validation is still a long way off. Published in *Cell Stem Cell*; raw data publicly available on Open Science Framework.

**Source type: Peer-reviewed paper / Evidence stage: Cell experiments + animal models / Credibility: High** ✅

**2. [One Drop of Blood, 130 Proteins — Lancet Study Reveals Why Alzheimer's Drugs Hit Differently for Different People](https://longevity.technology/news/blood-clues-to-why-alzheimers-drug-results-vary/)**

Washington University School of Medicine ran a tight protocol: 25 microliters of plasma per test — roughly a single drop — measuring 130 proteins to track patients on lecanemab (an anti-amyloid antibody therapy). 🔬 The findings were surprising: the protein markers tied to clearing amyloid from the brain and the ones tied to maintaining cognition were on completely separate trajectories. That means "clearing amyloid" and "preserving memory" may be controlled by different biological mechanisms — which goes a long way toward explaining why the same drug produces wildly different outcomes across patients. The study also found that brain-derived tau protein variants were far better at distinguishing treatment outcomes than peripheral blood tau. The catch: these biomarkers still can't predict who will benefit *before* treatment starts — and that's the answer clinical practice actually needs. Published in *Lancet Neurology*; detection platform provided by Alamar Biosciences (commercial interest noted).

**Source type: Peer-reviewed paper (observational longitudinal cohort) / Evidence stage: Human blood sample analysis / Credibility: Medium-high** 🔬

**3. [An AI Agent "Hopping" Across 1.6M Molecular Scaffolds — MolHop Charts a New Course for Drug Design](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.19920)**

Traditional AI drug design is basically "fine-tuning" known molecules — swap a few atoms, tweak a bond here or there. MolHop does something fundamentally different. ⚗️ It organizes the scaffolds of ~1.6M commercial molecules into a hierarchical map from coarse to fine, then lets an AI agent use reinforcement learning to *navigate* that map — finding candidates with completely different backbones but similar function. The real-world payoff for pharma R&D is direct: dodge existing patents and explore a much broader chemical space. MolHop-9B beats baseline models including GPT-5.6-sol and Claude-5-Opus on cross-scaffold tasks. That said, this is an arXiv preprint — benchmarking is on standard datasets, and it hasn't been validated in a real drug pipeline yet.

**Source type: arXiv preprint, not peer-reviewed / Evidence stage: Computational benchmark / Credibility: Medium** ⚠️

**4. [XPRIZE Bets $1M on Mitochondria — Mighty Therapeutics Enters the Healthspan Finals](https://longevity.technology/news/xprize-hands-1m-to-a-mitochondrial-drug-team/)**

Mitochondria are the cell's power plants — and they degrade with age. ⚡ Mighty Therapeutics and a University of Washington team just picked up a $1M milestone award from the $101M XPRIZE Healthspan competition based on early trial data for elamipretide, a peptide that specifically binds a critical mitochondrial lipid. The team will use the prize money to design a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to test whether it can improve muscle function, cognition, and immune function in older adults. Results expected around 2029; the "preliminary data" details haven't been published yet.

**5. [Reservoir Launches: The Cellino Team Wants to Bank Your Immune Cells Today for Tomorrow's Therapies](https://longevity.technology/news/cellino-team-launches-immune-cell-banking-service/)**

Reservoir's pitch is straightforward: one standard blood draw — 60mL — to extract a mixed population of T cells, B cells, natural killer cells, and more, then store them at -150°C indefinitely. 🧊 The logic from the Cellino team behind the service: immune cells degrade with age; naive T cells decline, mutation-carrying clones expand. Cells banked at 35 aren't the same as cells at 65. The service currently operates through partner clinics in Boston, San Francisco, Los Angeles, and New York. Operator Cell Harbor runs under the PHS Act Section 361 framework — "minimal manipulation," no pre-market approval needed. Cellino has raised over $96M from Khosla Ventures and others, plus up to $25M from ARPA-H. Key open question: will banked cells be sufficient in quantity for future cell therapies? Can expansion tech keep up? No answers yet. The service explicitly positions itself as "preservation only — not diagnostic, not therapeutic."

**6. [Can AI Reproduce Other Labs' Biotools on Its Own? ReproBAIT Gives Us a First Look](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.19810)**

An AI coding agent — no internet access, just training data, a compute budget, and a brief description of the tool — can it reproduce biotools like ProteinMPNN (protein inverse folding) or RFdiffusion3 (all-atom generative diffusion) from scratch? 🤖 The ReproBAIT benchmark framework delivers the first numbers: simple tools can be reproduced to a meaningful degree; complex ones, not so much. Researchers also found moderate evidence of "memorization" — the model may have seen these open-source tools' code during pretraining, potentially inflating reproduction performance. Practical implication for pharma and biotool developers: if you're thinking about publicly releasing tool details, you may need to rethink your trade secret protection and dual-use control strategy. arXiv preprint, pending peer review.

**Source type: arXiv preprint / Evidence stage: Benchmark study / Credibility: Medium** ⚠️

**7. [How Much Patient Privacy Leaks from Brain MRI Embeddings? More Than Anyone Realized](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.20494)**

A lot of medical AI research swaps out raw MRI images for model-generated "embedding vectors" before sharing data — the assumption being that this protects privacy. But does it? 🔐 This study tested 5 pretrained brain MRI encoders, and the results are uncomfortable: on the MIRIAD dataset, embeddings alone re-identified patient samples across time points with up to 99% accuracy; on OASIS-2, it exceeded 90%. Even more striking: predicting sex from embeddings hit balanced accuracy of 90–95%. The conclusion is clear — replacing raw images with embeddings alone is not sufficient for anonymization. This has direct implications for biomedical data sharing and privacy compliance. Preprint submitted, pending peer review.

**Source type: arXiv preprint / Evidence stage: Experimental privacy attack evaluation on public datasets / Credibility: Medium-high** 🔐

**9. [Texas Imaging Chain Brings FDA-Cleared Brain Function EEG Testing Into Routine Checkups](https://longevity.technology/news/texas-imaging-chain-adds-a-brain-function-test/)**

MRI and CT can show you what a brain *looks* like — they can't directly tell you how well it's *working*. 📡 Texas imaging chain DICOT is partnering with Toronto's VoxNeuro to integrate their cognitive function neuroimaging (cfNI) software into standard exams. **10. [AI Tracks Disease Trajectories in EHRs, Cuts Long-Range Timing Error by 19 Days](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.20502)**

Can you learn how diseases evolve step-by-step from hundreds of thousands of real hospital records? 📈 This study used graph neural networks to extract patients' "continuous hospitalization transition patterns" from the MIMIC-IV dataset, then combined them with a temporal Transformer to predict disease progression timing. For events beyond 730 days out, timing error dropped by ~59 days compared to baseline. If tools like this ever reach the clinic, they could support risk stratification for aging-related chronic disease progression. The paper is honest about limitations though: on macro C-index and overall Brier score, the method didn't outperform pure discrete history-based risk models — and short-term (sub-730-day) timing predictions actually got worse. arXiv preprint; MIMIC-IV is a research-grade dataset with real gaps vs. clinical deployment.

**Source type: arXiv preprint / Evidence stage: Retrospective computational study, validated on public dataset / Credibility: Medium** ⚠️
