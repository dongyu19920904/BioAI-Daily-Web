## **🔥 Today's Top 10 Focus**

### 1. Percival vision-language model extracts cross-organ health signals from CT scans

According to [Lifespan.io's coverage](https://lifespan.io/ai-extracts-broader-health-insights-from-routine-ct-scans/), **Percival**, a vision-language model developed by the University of Pennsylvania team, was trained on over 400,000 CT image volumes from **52,000+ participants** (npj Digital Medicine). The model embeddings showed significant associations with **35 lab markers** (including kidney function, liver function, blood glucose, lipids, and inflammation markers) and correlated with 351 cross-organ diagnostic categories; it outperformed image-only baseline models in predicting subsequent heart failure, cirrhosis, acute kidney injury, and other diseases. Researchers note that some "future diagnoses" may be existing but undocumented conditions, so the predictive capability here differs from genuine early warning years in advance. The study has not been directly compared to radiologists, so no conclusion can be drawn that Percival outperforms routine clinical imaging interpretation.

### 2. Tessera: spatial transcriptomics representation learning framework with directional sensitivity

[Paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.28579) shows that **Tessera** introduces geometric inductive bias into spatial transcriptomics representation learning while preserving directional information of inter-cell relationships rather than modeling distance alone. The framework employs a translation-invariant design and outperforms existing spatial foundation models across multiple tissues and sequencing technologies, with zero-shot generalization to contexts **16× larger** than training maximum. Currently a preprint; specific datasets and evaluation metrics await peer review, and causal mechanisms cannot yet be concluded.

### 3. HistoLoop: iterative spatial gene expression prediction from histopathology slides

[Paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.28339) introduces **HistoLoop**, a looped Transformer that iteratively refines spot states in H&E images to predict spatial gene expression. Under the HEST-Bench HVG50 evaluation protocol, HistoLoop achieved the highest macro-average gene Pearson correlation across **6 cancer tasks in 5 organs on the HEST-1K dataset**. This is observational prediction; it's a preprint and has not yet been validated for actual impact on clinical diagnosis or treatment decisions.

### 4. Brain2Vec: brain structure foundation model pretrained on 74,425 T1-weighted MRIs

[Paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.28411) shows that **Brain2Vec**, when initialized with pretrained weights for brain age prediction, outperforms random initialization in both final accuracy and convergence speed; frozen embeddings beat anatomical volume feature baselines in 4 out of 5 classification tasks. Model code and trained weights are open-sourced (see [CBICA/Brain2Vec](https://github.com/CBICA/Brain2Vec)) and also accessible via the NiChart cloud platform. Pretraining data aggregated from multiple studies; downstream tasks are all academic evaluations and not yet validated in clinical deployment.

### 5. Alterity's post-hoc reanalysis of MSA drug ATH434 reports 52% slowing of functional decline

According to [Longevity.Technology's coverage](https://longevity.technology/news/alterity-reanalysis-puts-msa-drug-at-52-slowing/), **Alterity Therapeutics** presented a post-hoc reanalysis of its phase-2 trial at the 2026 Seoul International Parkinson and Movement Disorder Congress (as a poster), adjusting baseline disease severity by cerebrospinal fluid **neurofilament light chain (NfL)**; the 50 mg group showed **4.64 fewer points** of worsening on the disability scale at Week 52 versus placebo (p=0.032; minimal clinically important difference for the scale is 1.5 points). Key limitations: the statistical model was added after trial initiation, the original primary endpoint (substantia nigra iron deposition) was not met, and the 75 mg dose did not reach statistical significance; this figure comes from exploratory reanalysis and does not equal confirmed clinical efficacy.

### 6. VGL-stGen: multimodal large language model for lung histopathology and spatial transcriptomics

[Paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.28266) introduces **VGL-stGen**, which starts from a pretrained medical vision-language model and combines paired lung H&E images, spatial transcriptomics, and single-cell reference data to support 13 tasks (tissue classification, cell type classification, gene prediction, and conditional expression imputation). Image permutation experiments show that correctly paired tissue images contributed additional information beyond observed expression. Researchers explicitly note that external classification depends on computational references sharing annotated lineages, and that some H&E sources were already exposed during training; results should be extrapolated cautiously.

### 7. GCLDock: curriculum learning improves protein-ligand flexible docking success rates

[Paper index/abstract](https://papers.cool/arxiv/2610.28145) proposes introducing curriculum learning into flexible docking training, progressively adding samples with large protein conformational changes. On the PDBBind ESMFold apo benchmark, **GCLDock** achieved a top-1 success rate of **46.52%** (ligand RMSD < 2Å), and 40.26% with additional PoseBusters validity constraints, surpassing existing baselines. This task targets protein-ligand binding pose prediction; it's a computational benchmark evaluation and has not been independently validated in wet-lab experiments or drug discovery pipelines.

### 8. Duke dementia AI tools clinical translation initiative

According to [News-Medical's coverage](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261021/Duke-initiative-aims-to-bring-dementia-AI-tools-into-clinical-practice.aspx), **Duke University** has launched an initiative aimed at bringing dementia AI tools into routine clinical practice, noting that while many research findings exist, few tools have actually been deployed. Specific tool names, evaluation metrics, enrolled populations, and pilot scale are all at the short-abstract level; detailed data awaits verification, and no inference can be made about clinical diagnostic accuracy or improved patient outcomes.
