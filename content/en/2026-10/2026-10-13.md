## **🔥 Top 10 Stories**

**1. [ZNF865 Anti-Senescence Gene May Fight Spinal Disc Deterioration](https://lifespan.io/an-anti-senescence-gene-may-fight-spinal-disc-deterioration/)**

ZNF865 is a little-known gene that might hold the key to one of the most widespread sources of chronic pain on the planet. Back pain affects hundreds of millions of people globally, but most treatments just mask the pain rather than stopping disc degeneration itself. In this *Aging Cell* study, researchers used CRISPR to upregulate ZNF865 in degenerated disc cells — senescence markers dropped back toward normal levels, and chromatin state differences shrank by 94%. In rats, silencing the gene triggered visible disc degeneration in under two months. The limitations are clear though: the molecular mechanism is still unknown, and beyond CRISPR there's no known drug that can directly target ZNF865. Human efficacy remains unproven.

Source type: Peer-reviewed paper / Evidence stage: Cell + animal experiments / Credibility: Medium

**2. [Buck Institute and Georgetown Score $51.7M to Map Protein Misfolding Before Disease Strikes](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20261012/Buck-Institute-and-Georgetown-lead-effort-mapping-harmful-protein-changes-before-disease-begins.aspx)**

Buck Institute for Research on Aging, teaming up with Georgetown University, just landed up to $51.7M from ARPA-H to tackle one of neuroscience's biggest timing problems. In diseases like Alzheimer's and Parkinson's, proteins start misfolding silently long before any symptoms show — by the time a patient walks into a clinic, the damage is often irreversible. ARPA-H is betting on that early window: the funding will go toward systematically mapping how proteins misfold before disease truly begins, and developing new diagnostic tools and treatments from that map. This is one of the rare large-scale, government-directed funding pushes aimed squarely at the core questions of longevity science. That said, it's still early days — actual diagnostic products and therapies are years away.

Source type: Official media report / Evidence stage: Research funding launch / Credibility: High

**3. [Age-Related CMA Decline Leaves Immune Cells Unable to Clear Senescent Cells](https://www.fightaging.org/archives/2026/10/age-related-decline-in-chaperone-mediated-autophagy-impairs-clearance-of-senescence-cells/)**

Chaperone-mediated autophagy (CMA) — a cellular cleanup mechanism that handles "problem proteins" — is the surprising culprit in this open-access paper. As we age, the immune system gets progressively worse at clearing accumulated senescent cells, but the *why* has been murky. This research points to CMA function declining in senescent cells with age, which then further suppresses CMA in macrophages (the immune system's cleanup crew), creating a vicious cycle. In mouse models, pharmacologically activating CMA reduced senescent cell accumulation *and* lowered pulmonary fibrosis severity. It's a promising new drug target for promoting senescent cell clearance — but the gap between mouse results and human treatment is still substantial.

Source type: Peer-reviewed paper (open access) / Evidence stage: Animal experiments / Credibility: Medium

**4. [11.4 Million New Dementia Cases in 2023 — Projected to More Than Double to 24.7 Million by 2050](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-10-global-dementia-reveals-million-cases.html)**

This isn't a projection model built from assumptions — it's one of the largest real-world incidence datasets on dementia ever assembled. The *JAMA Neurology* study pooled data from 22 population studies across 18 countries and regions, covering 38,418 participants, and landed on an estimate of roughly 11.4 million new dementia cases worldwide in 2023. The projected figure for 2050 tops 24.7 million. What makes this number matter is that it's a comparable baseline — whether you're in aging research or AI drug discovery, you need a reliable denominator to evaluate whether any intervention is actually working. This is observational research only; it doesn't touch on any treatment approaches.

Source type: Peer-reviewed observational study (multi-center) / Evidence stage: Epidemiological data / Credibility: High
